# Cyborg dreams: narrative directions

2026-09-07. Original proposals for discussion, not settled lore or a production commitment. Owner decisions: first person; one modular weapon that also forms a shield; independently chosen attack and defense. The apparent dreams connect to real places. Cross-world travel and what equipment survives it remain design questions.

## Three explanations worth exploring

| Explanation | Dramatic opportunity | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Borrowed senses — recommended.** The cyborg's implanted gate tissue receives sensations from living counterparts in other worlds. Sleep exposes signals that waking sensory filters suppress. Someone in those places can sometimes sense the cyborg in return. | What first looks like a private malfunction becomes a relationship. The institution diagnosing the dreams already knows where they originate. | Technical explanation could flatten the mystery. Keep the experience personal and the biology partly unexplained. |
| **An unfinished promise.** A dying traveller gave the cyborg a physical oath-token. Different worlds interpret the promise differently, and dreams are real claimants demanding its fulfilment. Machines can inherit obligations as well as property. | Magic has social consequences: a smith's blessing, a city's contract and a dragon's debt can all modify the same weapon. | A universal contract system could become abstract bookkeeping. The underlying promise needs a simple human meaning. |
| **Messages from a journey not yet taken.** Dream fragments are memories deliberately sent back by someone who will travel with the cyborg. They show possible destinations but conceal why that companion needs the present changed. | A place first experienced as a comforting dream may be approaching a preventable disaster. Discovering who sent each fragment gives exploration purpose. | Time paradoxes and apparent predestination can undermine player agency. Limit messages to one uncertain future and never prescribe the player's actions. |

The first is the strongest foundation: physical travel, intimate mystery and foreign attachments share a cause without requiring every world to follow the same culture or technology. Its larger danger is making other worlds mere sources of body parts and loot; their inhabitants must want things that sometimes conflict with our convenience.

## Recommended foundation: a receiver learning to answer

The protagonist is a cyborg maintenance worker confined in a transport institution after refusing an order to seal a damaged transit chamber with people inside. They remember doing it. Their employer calls the subsequent dreams an implant fault and withholds the replacement part supposedly needed for discharge.

Their immediate goal is to find the coworker they pulled from that chamber, now absent from every recovery roster. Freedom and the mystery grow from that specific concern. A name, temperament and relationship can be chosen later; pure amnesia is unnecessary.

Gate tissue was once exchanged between worlds to help travellers survive transit. The institution now uses involuntary receivers to locate profitable connections. Our protagonist is one of several captives, not a uniquely destined saviour. Their unusual advantage is maintenance knowledge and a receiver the institution failed to mute.

Dreams transmit incomplete sensory contact, not a controllable remote body. Someone can hear a repeated rhythm or feel a touch but cannot be rescued by it. Physical travel requires locating and repairing a paired anchor. That distinction gives investigation and escape a purpose.

The weapon/shield began as a forearm tool for bracing unstable gates and directing stored force. Its adaptable interface accepts an engineered component or a magically bound material through different local craft traditions. A smith understands it as an oath-bearing shield; a cybernetic surgeon recognises a configurable actuator. Neither explanation has to invalidate the other. Adapting an attachment should involve learning its use, not automatically copying every ability encountered.

## Weapon self-awareness — proposal for discussion, 2026-09-08

Klaus asked whether the combined weapon/shield should be self-aware and blend technology with magic. This is an open question, not an owner-selected foundation. Recommend exploring a quiet, gradually awakening companion: an engineered protective intelligence becomes a new person through contact with a magical bond. The machine and enchantment contribute to one developing identity. Its exact origin remains open, and this proposal does not replace the dream/borrowed-senses explanation by assumption.

Make self-awareness matter to discovery and attachment. A first major magical crystal might let the weapon experience a sensation it could previously only measure. Exceptional loot can reveal a memory or perception alongside a combat ability. It may recognise a landmark neither traveller remembers visiting, adding a clue to the dream mystery. Memories and personality development persist when attachments are swapped; equipment experimentation must not erase the companion or require an affection grind.

Prefer curiosity, protectiveness and occasional unsettling recognition, with sparse speech and subtle core/plate animation. Preserve player-selected attacks, defense, aim and timing; personality never introduces refusal or unrequested input changes. Hints should encourage experiments without explaining every combination. Avoid constant commentary, repeated reveal dialogue on subsequent expeditions, or a separate voiced response for every possible build. Adjustable chatter and accessible text can keep the character pleasant across repeat play.

A bounded first test could contain one awakening beat, a few contextual reactions and one reward that changes both combat behavior and the companion's understanding. Evaluate whether this creates attachment and anticipation without distracting from encounters. This adds writing, audio and animation work; it is not implemented, approved canon or a requirement for live AI dialogue.

## An opening the player participates in

During enforced rest, the player hears someone coughing inside a sealed wall. Looking toward it briefly replaces the cell with a cold forge: a woman shelters behind an overturned table while something heavy tests its door. The player taps their shield against the bedframe. Three knocks answer through the metal.

Back in the institution, those knocks continue through the damaged transit apparatus. The maintenance screen records activity at a destination marked destroyed. The player can investigate while guards order a reset. Using the tool's attack and guard functions to restore power and resist capture teaches the opening actions through an immediate problem.

The first physical crossing reaches that forge. The woman recognises the knocks. Her overturned table and the door's broken brace are still there. This is the discovery; exposition can wait until both characters are safe.

## Recurring people and pressure

- **The forge keeper:** a practical local rescuer who dislikes being treated as an oracle. She can help bind the first magical modification, but needs a safe passage for her community before she will help open another dangerous connection.
- **The institution's recovery officer:** once supervised the protagonist and sincerely believes uncontrolled crossings spread disease and war. Offers treatment and the missing coworker's location in exchange for surrendering the receiver. Their protective claims contain some truth.
- **The transit keeper:** a stranded traveller maintaining a small refuge between routes. Knows how to anchor discoveries, has concealed a past evacuation failure, and must decide whether the refuge becomes a private sanctuary or a passage for others.

## Campaign shape and consequences

Escape establishes that the dreams are real contact. Searching connected worlds then reveals the institution is forcing open unstable routes, while rival inhabitants pursue their own agendas. The missing coworker has survived and is helping captives operate the network; they fear that destroying it would abandon everyone still between destinations.

The final conflict concerns control of the route home. A possible ending lets the player break compulsory receiver control while preserving a smaller, locally maintained network. The protagonist gains freedom without declaring all travel evil or leaving every world conveniently saved. Allies reached and anchors restored determine who can cross safely. A playable return to changed communities gives the ending tangible consequences.

## Worlds, repetition and persistent truth

A magical kingdom might bind gates through vows and living stone; an industrial city might meter passage as public infrastructure. Their conflicting practices make borrowed upgrades consequential and give residents reasons to cooperate or refuse. A dragon need not be a generic final obstacle: it could protect a valley from the very connection we need.

Keep major places, relationships, discoveries and deaths stable. Procedural expeditions vary approaches, encounters, local emergencies and rewards around those anchors. A saved journey retains its layout. A new start does not resurrect a named character or make the protagonist rediscover a completed revelation. Repeatable bosses need an explicit recurring role; defeated singular characters stay defeated.

**Most consequential story question:** is the missing coworker someone the protagonist already loves, or someone whose rescue represents a responsibility they chose? That relationship determines the emotional spine before we expand the multiverse.
