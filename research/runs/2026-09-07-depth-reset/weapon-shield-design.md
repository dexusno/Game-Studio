# Weapon, shield and reward design

**2026-09-08 owner correction after beta2:** the shield itself must be the physical weapon, including launch/recall and meaningful attack/defense commitment. Klaus rejected the implemented gun-with-guard interpretation. Freely chosen close attacks/guarding must remain viable; the old compulsory shoot-everything/recall rhythm stays rejected. The pulse/frame examples below are historical proposals, not the current baseline. Resume from the [game brief](../../../games/dreambound/BRIEF.md) and [playtest correction](../../../games/dreambound/RETROSPECTIVE.md).

2026-09-07. **Design proposal, unimplemented and untested.** This refines the owner's request for engaging combat, extensive ability-changing attachments, meaningful loot and achievements, procedural biomes, story and visual identity. Klaus subsequently selected **first person and one modular weapon that forms a shield, with freely chosen attack and defense**. The [integrated cyborg/worlds proposal](cyborg-worlds-design.md) adds his dream-of-real-worlds premise and compares carryover rules. The rejected Sixfold beta remains parked. Labels are working descriptions, not cleared game or product names.

## The player promise

Start with a capable weapon and shield, learn to use them well, and discover attachments that change how they work together. A run should develop from a recognizable starting idea into a build shaped by discoveries and decisions. Defeating an enemy, earning an attachment, clearing a level and beating a boss should each produce a distinct, understandable payoff. Campaign achievements persist while expeditions vary.

The leading test is: **does a reward change a decision in the next fight, and can a later reward make the player reconsider a successful plan?** Counting items or increasing completion speed is insufficient evidence. A large eventual catalog is desirable only when its interactions remain comprehensible and produce worthwhile choices.

**Owner refinement, 2026-09-07:** a large combinable attachment/magic catalog and genuinely overpowered builds are intentional design goals. Creative assembly, challenging acquisition and smart execution should be able to produce extraordinary results. A successful combination may wipe a room or overwhelm a boss. Earlier caution about dominance must not be interpreted as flattening every build to equal strength or forbidding exciting reaction chains.

## Start with satisfying actions

The base kit supplies aimed attacks, an alternate attack or punish opportunity, useful directional guard, timed defense and movement/evasion. It must already answer mandatory enemy roles without waiting for rare loot. Holding guard provides protection within its readable limits; timing it well gives a stronger opportunity. Telegraph which attacks need evasion. Essential responsiveness and basic survivability are present from the start.

Attacking does not inherently require discarding every shield component. The earlier mandatory all-at-once throw/recall rhythm is not the foundation of this proposal. Optional deployable or expendable attachments may create a clearly chosen offense/defense tradeoff while retaining a useful baseline response. The previous shared-charge hypothesis is now one possible build mechanism, not a compulsory sequence for all builds.

Ordinary offensive and defensive actions remain freely selectable. Most attachments modify these existing actions rather than adding another hotkey. Exact guard, cancel, recovery, aim and resource rules need a combat prototype. Test how it feels without upgrades before relying on the reward loop to carry it.

## An extensible equipment structure

These are functional categories, not a final inventory layout. Capacity is limited so a build has an identity and selecting one transformation has a cost. The eventual catalog can grow within these categories without equipping everything simultaneously.

| Layer | What it changes | Proposed examples |
|---|---|---|
| Weapon frame | Base attack rhythm and range | A deliberate piercing shot; a short-range spread with a committed impact; a curved projectile that rewards aiming around cover |
| Weapon mechanism | Delivery or spatial behavior | Split after a direct hit, bounce from marked surfaces, place a tether, echo an attack after a delay |
| Shield attachment | Defensive behavior and its offensive opportunities | Capture a countershot, plant a barrier panel, commit to a shield rush, deploy intercepting spines |
| Elemental core | A readable manipulation of enemies or space | Conductive marks, chill and shatter, controlled fire zones, gravity compression |
| Interaction catalyst | A specific connection between two actions or effects | Fire through a planted panel, turn a shatter into a storm arc, use timed defense to empower an aimed follow-up |

Frames and starting attachments can become permanent choices. A run fills or changes remaining slots and acquires temporary amplifiers. Basic statistical tuning can support a design, but headline attachments need a recognizable behavioral effect. A rarer object may offer an unusual transformation with a tradeoff rather than automatically superseding every ordinary item.

The owner's latest refinement treats the weapon/shield as the central evolving object, with technology, magic, crystals, jewelry and possible stackable upgrades. The [attachment catalogue](attachment-catalogue.md), added 2026-09-08, develops 120 component and enhancement proposals: 100 behavior/rule concepts and 20 supporting enhancement types. It distinguishes quantity stacks, supporting strength/rate stacks and behavioral evolution. This is a design pool, not 120 validated independent abilities or a release commitment; ranks do not inflate the ability count. Exact capacity, evolution thresholds and which improvements persist remain open.

## Earned power and discovery

Build components around shared behaviors: an impact can consume chill, a consumed chilled state can create a shatter, a shatter can trigger an arc, a manually released countershot can inherit compatible attack modifiers. These relationships should work wherever their actual conditions hold, rather than recognizing only named equipment recipes. Explain the individual effects clearly and leave room for the player to discover their combinations.

The catalog should span delivery patterns, capture and storage, deployment, movement, elemental states, conversion and reaction behavior. Avoid filling it with copies distinguished mainly by a percentage or rarity color. The earlier beta sample is not a ceiling on the eventual catalog. Its growth needs both broad interoperability and distinct applications, with actual playable evidence before making a numerical variety claim.

| Example combination | Desired extraordinary payoff | What makes it earned |
|---|---|---|
| Frost core + Ram Edge + Stormfracture | Chill a formation, rush into an impact, and create a shatter/lightning cascade capable of clearing the room. | Learn the components' relationship, obtain the rare catalyst, group/chill enemies and commit at a useful moment. Conductive marks may improve routing but are not a missing prerequisite. |
| Anchor Foot + Gate Lens + Ember core | Feed attacks through a deliberately positioned panel to create an intense elemental firing lane that can melt a boss during an opening. | Acquire complementary parts, choose an angle and sustain the attack at the right time. This is player-fed fire; an autonomous emitter would require its own mechanism. |
| Mirror Facet + Echo Chamber + a boss capacitor | Capture a dangerous volley and manually release an amplified counter-barrage with a delayed second strike. | A boss-earned catalyst expands storage/release behavior; the player still has to catch useful fire, retain the payload and aim the release. Echo recognizes a manually released counterattack as an attack. |

These illustrate relationships, not fixed classes or the only accepted combinations. The boss capacitor occupies the catalyst category, distinct from Mirror's shield attachment and Echo's weapon mechanism. Existing slot compatibility must remain explicit. Its exact capacity and acquisition encounter are not tuned or implemented.

Put difficulty in understanding, earning and assembling the parts, route commitments and execution. Rare components can come from a difficult boss, an elite hunt, a hidden location reached through observation, or an optional challenge with a disclosed reward. A desired part should eventually have a knowable pursuit, not require endless lucky rerolls. Useful ordinary components and a satisfying baseline kit remain available before the exceptional combination comes together.

Do not attach a cancelling drawback to every exciting reward. Earlier choices, acquisition and finite equipped capacity can supply the cost; a rare capstone may be an outright payoff. A veteran who deliberately equips an earned powerful combination is allowed to feel powerful. Different geography, objectives and tempting discoveries should encourage alternatives without punishing a favorite build or confiscating ownership. Some clearly identified temporary amplifiers can remain expedition-specific; that distinction must be understood when acquired.

### Make a major reward tempting to try

Give boss rewards a recognizable connection to a witnessed ability. A guardian that banks incoming fire can yield the capacitor that expands the player's captured barrage. An elite that causes violent fractures can introduce Stormfracture. Hidden loot can reveal an unusual way to route or position an attack. These are design examples, not new promises about the first beta's enemy inventory.

The reward moment should communicate a new possibility through its name, physical installation, animation, sound and a short effect explanation. Provide a nearby safe opportunity to inspect/test the change and a subsequent encounter where it can matter. A major reward choice should include an immediately applicable option; other discoveries can establish an appealing future pursuit. Do not interrupt the payoff with an unrelated second crafting grind. Preserve learned ownership according to the stated claim rules.

For a concrete beta test, the player earns Frost and Ram through play, then finds Stormfracture after a demanding elite. Explain that shattering creates chain lightning without prescribing an exact recipe. Let the player try grouping and chilling enemies, execute the impact and enjoy a dramatic wipe. Later offer a boss opening that the resulting build can exploit heavily. Observe installation, experimentation, understanding and desire for another component, alongside actual combat behavior. A deliberately successful power payoff is not a balance failure merely because the fight becomes short.

### Protect the game without suppressing combinations

Allow secondary effects to trigger compatible effects. Track the originating attack, payload, applied/consumed state and spent resources so an event cannot endlessly re-trigger itself or spend the same stored charge repeatedly. Use bounded processing and defined repeated-effect behavior to keep cascades executable and readable; these are not automatic boss damage caps. Resource-positive chains may be legitimate where they follow the rules and involve actual targets/actions. Test sustained and extreme cases instead of disabling all such interactions in advance.

Do not silently raise enemy resistance or add invulnerability solely to cancel a working build. A well-earned burst may end an encounter or shorten a boss fight substantially. Review a specific interaction if it is universally dominant with negligible acquisition/setup/execution, causes nonterminating effects, breaks saves, or makes further choices irrelevant. Distinguish those findings from the intended pleasure of briefly overwhelming opposition. Optional harder challenges can offer somewhere else to exercise mastery; they do not have to erase the victory just earned.

**Independent review:** the designer identified a hidden Stormfracture prerequisite in the earlier wording and an overly broad restriction on secondary triggers. The revised rule below works with any qualifying shatter, and controlled effect chains remain available. The review also noted that no system can force a fresh playstyle while giving full freedom to repeat favorites. We should offer worthwhile new possibilities and observe voluntary experimentation.

## Sample attachment vocabulary

This is a design palette, not a promise to implement every item in the first beta. Numerical tuning remains open. Mechanisms with overlapping roles should be merged or cut if play does not distinguish them.

| Attachment | Ability and player decision | Limitation that preserves a decision |
|---|---|---|
| Forking Prism | A qualifying hit splits the shot into a controlled spread behind that target. Line up formations. | Prevent unbounded self-splitting within one attack; child hits retain compatible elemental/reaction interactions. |
| Ricochet Rail | Deliberately bounce a shot from designated surfaces to reach another angle. | Limited bounces with a readable preview; no universal enemy-seeking behavior. |
| Tether Bolt | Mark a light enemy and use a subsequent deliberate movement/activation to pull it across a lane. | One active tether with range and mass limits; heavy targets get a different bounded response. |
| Echo Chamber | Leave a delayed repeat of an aimed attack, including compatible manually released counterfire, at its recorded position/direction. | It can inherit other compatible effects; prevent endlessly echoing itself or claiming the same spent resource again. |
| Mirror Facet | A timed defense banks captured fire that the player manually aims and releases. | Supported payload behavior and storage must be explicit; powerful captured fire can remain powerful. Storage/release attachments can expand the payoff. |
| Anchor Foot | Detach a shield plate to form a placed cover panel and a useful firing angle. | A narrower handheld guard remains; the panel has limited durability and cannot solve flank or ground threats. |
| Ram Edge | Turn the shield punish into a committed short rush that separates or staggers susceptible enemies. | Recovery and readable resistant attacks make timing important. |
| Orbit Spines | Deploy a small orbiting screen to intercept limited shots and threaten nearby attackers while moving. | Limited duration/interceptions; it leaves identifiable gaps and does not automate all defense. |
| Storm Conductor | Timed defense stores a spark; the next deliberate attack spends it to arc through a marked target. | Competes with other catalysts; the arc cannot replenish its own spark. Ordinary attacks remain useful. |
| Stormfracture | A qualifying shatter releases chain lightning toward nearby targets; conductive marks can improve routing. | Consume the source state once. Further legitimate reactions are allowed; prevent repeated consumption and nonterminating chains. |
| Gate Lens | Firing through a planted shield panel changes the shot into a directed elemental beam. | The angle and panel position matter; relocating consumes an opportunity and leaves the player exposed to new approaches. |
| Backcast | After an evasive move, the next deliberate special can originate from the position left behind. | One recorded position, a short use window and no recursive relocation. |

One core provides a primary element. A catalyst can add a specifically defined secondary reaction, such as Stormfracture's arc, without granting every ability of a second core. Loot generation must understand these prerequisites and offer usable reinforcement or a workable change of direction.

## Elements must have observable behavior

| Core | What it does | Example interaction |
|---|---|---|
| Storm | Direct attacks establish conductive marks; a deliberate discharge routes between compatible nearby marks. | Place or choose targets so an arc reaches a protected support enemy. A planted panel can become a relay with the appropriate attachment. |
| Frost | Build a readable chilled state, then consume it with an impact to shatter or create an opening. | Hold a flanker briefly, approach safely, then use a shield rush or committed shot to break the formation. |
| Ember | Create small, readable burning zones and deliberately rupture marked embers. | Close one approach, bait a pursuer across it, and detonate when another threat arrives. |
| Gravity | Briefly compress or redirect susceptible targets toward a chosen point. | Group light enemies for a piercing shot or pull a flanker away from a safe retreat. Heavy targets resist displacement. |

Do not make a biome's main path require one random element. Boss control must use bounded reactions, such as a short stagger contribution or an exposed opening, instead of permanent freezing or arbitrary immunity to an entire build. Status feedback uses motion, shape and sound as well as color. Slow effects must preserve readable correspondence between animation and actual attack timing.

## Three contrasting build stories

| Build direction | Example equipment | How the player behaves | Pressure that keeps it active |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storm Duelist | Precision frame, Mirror Facet, Storm core, Storm Conductor | Read an aimed attack, counter it, choose which marked target to hit, then reposition for the next line. | Flankers and warned ground attacks demand movement; waiting to counter is not universally safe. |
| Glacier Breaker | Short-range frame, Ram Edge, Frost core, Stormfracture | Separate enemies, build chill on a priority threat, commit to an impact, and use the opening to pursue or retreat. | Spacing, recovery and resistant heavy attacks punish automatic rushes. |
| Prism Engineer | Precision or curved frame, Anchor Foot, Storm core, Gate Lens | Establish a panel angle, draw threats into a firing lane, then move the setup when the encounter changes. | Side entrances, relocating enemies and clearly signaled machinery hazards challenge static cover play. |

These are examples discovered through play, not fixed character classes or three guaranteed recipes. A reward might tempt the duelist to exchange counter storage for a placed panel, or the breaker to exchange burst payoff for a safer control option. Safe installation points should allow inspection and a brief trial before committing a replacement. Whether a run can carry a spare attachment is an interface/balance question for the prototype.

## Loot: a desired goal and an unexpected possibility

Use the [independent progression review](progression-review.md) as the detailed state and acquisition proposal. A known attachment can have a disclosed skill-based source. Pinning that pursuit reserves a reachable optional challenge with a previewed reward, so finding the challenge is not an unlimited lottery. Other routes, encounters and caches still offer discoveries that could change the plan. Early rewards must create useful options before boredom sets in; the eventual rare catalog must not hold all the interesting actions hostage.

**Concrete pursuit:** the journal says Storm Conductor comes from the Relay Trial. A generated route includes that optional trial and shows its reward. Complete it with the starting kit, permanently learn the plan, receive an installable copy and try it at the next encounter. If the expedition later ends in defeat, the plan remains an available starting choice within the normal equipment capacity. Repeating the trial offers a disclosed temporary benefit or optional mastery challenge, not duplicate permanent ownership or an endlessly efficient currency farm.

Keep known source, owned plan, current equipment and temporary amplification distinct. Preserve claimed rewards and world state when resuming; starting a new expedition creates a new setup. Acquisitions and claims need consistent saves to prevent reward duplication or losing an earned milestone after a crash.

Offer pools should consider equipment compatibility, biome identity and progression, without handing out a guaranteed perfect combo or burying wanted items in a growing global pool. Show enough information for an informed replacement: changed action, meaningful limitation, and an example interaction. A surprising alternative should be useful independently or come with an achievable path to usefulness.

## Make achievement tangible

| Milestone | Immediate payoff | Lasting meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Skilled exchange | Clear hit, deflection, stagger and recovery feedback | The player recognizes something they learned to do better. |
| Encounter clear | Pressure resolves, reward becomes accessible and a choice can affect the next encounter | A visible step toward the level objective; not every room needs another menu. |
| Desired attachment earned | Install it, see the physical change and use its new behavior promptly | Plan ownership persists; its acquisition goal is marked complete. |
| Level clear | The level objective resolves and the next destination becomes available | Campaign access, a useful starting option or another explicit milestone is saved. |
| First boss victory | A distinctive reward derived from the boss's abilities, a strong audiovisual finish and safe collection | A campaign milestone, visible trophy/world change and new equipment potential; save before later hazards. |
| Campaign ending | Resolve the main objective and show the consequences of the journey | Optional challenge routes and remaining discoveries invite further play after a real completion. |

Failure should make the lost temporary power and surviving achievements clear. Permanent advancement primarily opens possibilities and campaign access; avoid making repeated low-risk grinding the prerequisite for adequate damage or defense. Optional mastery rewards can recognize exceptional play without withholding baseline viability. A boss reward should be worth pursuing because the player can imagine using it.

## Procedural biomes, encounter design and story

Generate meaningful combinations of authored room functions, entrance angles, encounters, reward paths and optional objectives. Vary the opening problem as well as later layouts. Start from a selected equipment identity, offer early branching opportunities, and apply recent-repeat limits within the available content pool. Same-seed practice and save/resume should remain reproducible. A large seed count does not measure experiential variety.

Illustrative biome identities, all unbuilt:

| Biome direction | Spatial and tactical identity | Visual/story opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Storm foundry | Layered gantries, moving machinery, optional conductive relays and multiple approach heights | Weathered structure, glowing cores and dormant systems restored through major clears |
| Drowned conservatory | Alternating tight paths and open flooded courts, interrupted sightlines and moving living cover | Roots entwined with crafted machinery; equipment and discoveries reveal how it changed |
| Glass ravine | Long sightlines connected by sheltered crossings, deliberate reflective surfaces and threatened flanks | Fractured crystalline architecture and recognizable reflection landmarks |

Each biome needs varied encounter shapes so one loadout is not systematically invalidated. Required movement, resources and objectives remain reachable with the baseline kit. Named reward families can give route choices a purpose; optional elemental shortcuts must leave a viable ordinary route.

A first boss could combine an aimed salvo, a warned ground sweep and a close attack with distinct openings. The duelist can exploit the salvo, the engineer can use a panel while adapting to ground pressure, and the breaker can earn stagger during exposed windows. Baseline play remains viable. These behaviors need real animation, collision and encounter testing before calling the boss fair.

One possible story foundation is equipment whose lost functions are tied to the world's guardians. Earning a new function changes both the player's moves and what they understand about the world. Hub characters, machinery and routes react to completed milestones. Keep reveals, discoveries and room events governed by campaign state so shuffled spaces do not create contradictory story order. This is a narrative direction to evaluate, not a selected setting or a commitment to extensive cinematics.

For art, make attachments recognizable physical objects with different silhouettes, deployment motion, effects and sound. A reflective facet, an anchoring leg and a crystal core should communicate different functions even before reading a tooltip. Rich materials and lighting can support either magical craft or technology. Refine the final style and selected first-person presentation through gameplay-scale inspection; effects must preserve sight of the enemy and its tell. No finished visual assets are implied by this document.

## What the next beta must teach us

The earlier proposed item/enemy counts were starting quantities, not the governing quality target. Build enough of the system to test the full progression from capable starting kit to early reward, a changed fight, a second meaningful choice, a level/boss payoff, and a different subsequent start. At least two contrasting build paths must be obtainable through the biome rather than available only in a developer loadout menu. Include one disclosed attachment pursuit, saved ownership, failure/retry and several generated arrangements.

Verify baseline combat and contrasting builds against comparable mixed encounters. Record changed movement, target order, timing and use of defense. Check whether the player notices and understands the reward, can pursue a desired attachment, considers a surprising alternative, feels the boss victory earned, and voluntarily wants another expedition. Owner fun feedback remains necessary. Automated checks cover compatibility, reaction limits, saves, generation and collisions, not enjoyment.

Engine rules must distinguish direct actions from secondary effects while permitting compatible chains, define state/resource consumption and repeated control, and specify how replacing an attachment changes already active effects. Effect and resource limits should correspond to the displayed behavior without automatically flattening powerful combinations. Test ordinary, awkward and deliberately overpowered loadouts. Expanding the catalog comes after representative combinations survive these tests. The next action is to integrate this decision/reward sequence with the selected first-person combined weapon/shield and the cyborg/worlds design, then establish the concrete implementation brief. Equipment form and viewport have been chosen; do not ask for those decisions again.

## Evidence and independent challenge

The owner supplied the requirements directly on 2026-09-07. The specific equipment, acquisition and world proposals above are studio hypotheses. The separate [progression review](progression-review.md) challenged the design: collecting a predetermined recipe can repeat the original problem even when the catalog is large. This revision therefore requires reinforcement and reconsideration, meaningful early actions and an explicit acquisition/use/restart test.

One historical primary reference is Supergiant's [Hades High Speed Update notes](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-high-speed-update-patch-notes/), published 2019-06-14 with the major update dated 2019-06-11, accessed 2026-09-07. They document behavioral changes to shield movement, incompatible weapon upgrades, matching effects and fixes to upgrade interactions. The design inference is that behavior-changing upgrades require compatibility rules, clear feedback and iteration. This is not a current balance guide, a copied upgrade catalog, a sales claim or evidence our own design is fun.
