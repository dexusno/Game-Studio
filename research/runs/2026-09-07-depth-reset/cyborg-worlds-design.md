# The cyborg between worlds — integrated working design

2026-09-07. **Creative design in refinement; no new game implemented or playtested.** Owner decisions: first person; one modular weapon that also forms a shield, with freely chosen attack and defense; deep ability-changing upgrades and meaningful achievement; procedurally varied levels/runs. Owner story seed: a cyborg's dreams reveal real places, reachable after escaping its current confinement. Worlds may have radically different technology, magic and biomes. The exact explanation, story, art style, transfer rules and campaign scope remain proposals.

This integrates the owner-requested [narrative writer](dreamworld-narrative.md), [art director](dreamworld-art-direction.md) and [producer review](dreamworld-production-review.md) with the [weapon/shield design](weapon-shield-design.md) and [progression review](progression-review.md). The old Sixfold prototype remains parked. Earlier camera alternatives and the reactor-raider label are historical research context; do not ask the owner to choose the viewport or combined/separate equipment again.

## The promise to test

**Discover that the places you dream about are real, escape into them, and bring something extraordinary back in the weapon you are building.** Each world should offer a different problem and tempting new possibilities. Acquired abilities need to change aiming, positioning, timing, defense or route choice. Different scenery and larger numbers cannot carry this promise alone.

The cyborg, its equipment and the people encountered provide continuity. A new run varies the journey while preserving meaningful achievements. The campaign has developments and an ending; world generation supports revisiting and discovery afterward.

**Latest owner direction, 2026-09-07:** Klaus endorses the basic concept and wants a large combinable mod/magic catalog, including genuinely overpowered builds earned through creativity and smart combinations. Boss, elite and rare discoveries should introduce exciting abilities worth trying. The [earned-power design](weapon-shield-design.md) now specifies shared effect rules, example room-clearing/boss-burst combinations, acquisition and immediate-use rewards. Exceptional success is part of the intended experience; automatic power flattening is not.

## Recommended narrative: incomplete contact

The writer compared living cross-world receivers, inherited magical obligations and messages from a possible future. Recommend the first as the current working explanation. The cyborg's implant receives fragments of another person's senses during sleep. Its captors call this a malfunction while using other receivers to locate valuable destinations. The dreams are contact with actual people and places. Entering those places physically is necessary to help anyone or change events.

Give the protagonist a remembered responsibility: a maintenance worker who refused to seal a damaged transit chamber while people were inside. A missing coworker gives the escape a personal aim. The exact relationship is open; the protagonist need not be amnesiac or uniquely destined. Let the mystery unfold through evidence rather than explaining the implant in the introduction.

**Opening motif:** during confinement, a cold forge briefly replaces the cell. Someone shelters behind an overturned table while a heavy presence tests a door. The cyborg knocks against metal; three knocks answer. Later, a physical crossing leads to the same broken brace and table. The forge keeper recognizes the signal. After helping each other survive, she can bind the first magical attachment to the player's very physical machine.

A recovery officer believes unrestricted crossings can spread catastrophe; a stranded transit keeper maintains a refuge and has their own past failure. Inhabitants need things beyond being sources of loot. An eventual conflict can concern who controls the routes, with an ending that frees involuntary receivers while preserving some community-maintained connections. These characters, motivations and ending are proposals, not settled canon.

Procedural routes must respect persistent truth. Major locations, named relationships, discoveries and deaths remain consistent. Vary approaches, local emergencies, opposition and rewards around those anchors. After the first escape, subsequent expeditions bypass the completed introductory revelation. A singular defeated character stays defeated; a repeatable guardian needs a repeatable role in the fiction.

## One object across incompatible-looking worlds

The weapon/shield could originate as a gate-maintenance tool, designed to brace unstable passages and direct force. Its central spine and articulated plates stay recognizable. Attacks align a delivery channel; guarding fans selected plates into protection. The player chooses actions responsively, with no mandatory all-at-once discard and recall cycle.

Different cultures can adapt its interface through engineering or magical craft. A frost crystal sits in a machined collar; a cybernetic lens focuses a rune; an anchoring plate channels a captured spell. The story need not dismiss magic as misunderstood technology. The shared interface makes adaptation possible, while each attachment retains a particular behavior and limitation.

Use the equipment layers and examples in the weapon/shield proposal: frame, weapon mechanism, shield attachment, elemental core and interaction catalyst. This defines an extensible vocabulary rather than a giant first-beta inventory. Baseline attack, guard, timed defense and movement must be satisfying before collecting anything.

The [attachment catalogue](attachment-catalogue.md), added 2026-09-08, explores 120 component and enhancement ideas across weapon delivery, shield/movement, crystals, technology/runes, jewelry/pacts and supporting stacks. The owner wants the object to remain the hero of progression. Let cultures leave visible and functional changes on the same weapon; stacked copies can amplify an effect or feed a disclosed evolution. Permanent learned patterns and mastery must stay distinct from temporary expedition stacks. Catalogue size, sockets, ranks and transfer capacity are proposals, not implementation commitments.

## Carryover: three alternatives worth comparing

| Model | What it offers | Main problem |
|---|---|---|
| Carry the full build | Strong continuous ownership and immediate power fantasy | Later worlds can become tours for one established dominant build; combination and balance costs compound. |
| Rebuild the active loadout in each world | Strong local identity and fresh rewards | Earning a cherished upgrade can feel pointless if it is repeatedly taken away. |
| **Keep learned patterns; select active transfers — recommended** | Durable achievement, a real crossing decision and room for local discoveries | The allowance may feel arbitrary or force a favorite combination apart unless choice and storage are clear. |

Prototype the third model. The proposed fiction is that the transit interface can stabilize only some active foreign modifications during a crossing. This explanation must support an enjoyable rule; it cannot rescue a transfer limit that feels bad.

| State | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| Cyborg identity, basic weapon/shield actions and learned player skill | Always retained |
| Earned attachment patterns and major discoveries | Permanently retained under explicit acquisition rules |
| Selected portable attachments | Active on arrival, within a transfer allowance |
| Other earned attachments | Archived as owned options for later expedition preparation; clearly distinguish stored from destroyed |
| Temporary local amplifiers and consumables | Expire or transfer according to their displayed rule; preview this before crossing |
| Claimed boss rewards, story milestones and unlocked destinations | Saved durably; later failure does not undo their completion |

**Two portable attachments is an initial comparison setting, not a decided limit.** Re-evaluate the allowance against the owner's desired rich combinations: crossing should preserve a satisfying build foundation instead of routinely dismantling the payoff just earned. Compare a larger allowance or a different transfer model if necessary. Do not make the player carry basic attack and defense as optional slots. Choosing portable abilities commits that expedition's entry setup; local discoveries can still transform it. Permanent ownership can eventually support powerful starts, and repeating a favorite build remains a valid choice.

Example: arrive in a magical kingdom with a technological Tether Bolt and Anchor Foot, occupying different equipment categories. Acquire frost magic. At the next crossing, select the frost core and Anchor Foot for control and placed cover, or the frost core and Tether Bolt for pull-and-shatter opportunities. Everything learned remains owned. In the technological destination, frost can create an opening against a charging machine; a newly found Gate Lens could transform the panel build further. Transfer choices must respect active-slot compatibility and catalyst prerequisites, so the player never unknowingly carries a catalyst whose required attachment was left behind. The exact allowance needs a playable test.

Imported abilities must have useful, consistent roles. Define shared target properties and understandable reactions across worlds; a heavy guardian can resist being pulled without invalidating the entire gravity build. Mandatory encounters remain solvable with the base kit, while earned combinations may overwhelm them. Avoid secretly raising resistance just because an item crossed a portal or a build proved strong. Distinguish direct and secondary effects to support compatible chains and prevent nonterminating self-triggering, not to prohibit every powerful cascade.

## Earning, using and wanting the next thing

Offer both disclosed pursuits and surprising discoveries. A wanted attachment can come from a reachable optional challenge reserved by generation. Completing it teaches the pattern and grants something immediately usable. Other rewards can reinforce the current plan or invite a workable change. A growing catalog should not dilute every item into one enormous lottery.

A first boss victory should resolve an obstacle, grant a distinctive functional reward, reveal something about the world and unlock a destination or other campaign milestone. Save those achievements before subsequent danger. Let the player use the reward promptly, including across a crossing when appropriate. Repeating a safe first-room task must not become the fastest mandatory path to progression.

On defeat, clearly show what was learned and kept and which temporary expedition effects ended. Once destinations are unlocked, future expedition starts can use those opportunities rather than always replaying the same confinement. Selected starting equipment supplies agency; generated entry situations, routes and early offers supply variation. Resuming a save restores that expedition rather than rolling a new one.

## Worlds and the visual thread

The art director recommends richly modeled, painterly 3D with tactile surfaces and strong lighting. This is a style proposal, not a finished look. Keep the cyborg's central silhouette, wear, material response and weapon construction consistent. Make attachments visibly change its shape, deployment and energy behavior. First-person guarding must preserve a readable view of enemies; custom transitions and interruption behavior are central production work.

Three possible world families from the art exploration:

- **A medieval realm of roots, bells and living stone:** towers and flooded cloisters, sheltered paths opening onto exposed bridges, crafted magic and bindings. A dragon could have a role in the region's conflict; it is an aspiration beyond the first beta.
- **A cyberpunk transit district:** stacked maintenance passages, shutters and roof routes, surveillance and technological modifications. A bounded district offers a testable world identity without assuming a functioning metropolis.
- **A tidal archive:** black cliffs, stranded vessels and exposed causeways with contrasting shelter and sightlines. Authored tide configurations could change expeditions; a simulated ocean is not required.

Each needs its own encounter and route rules, not only different props. Shared controls, cues and interaction principles let the player learn across worlds. The widest production risk is that independently sourced packs look and play like unrelated demos. A consistent protagonist and deliberately unified materials/animation help, but each realm still requires real authored content.

## Representative beta proposal

The producer recommends **one substantial generated world and a compact playable arrival in a contrasting destination**. Integrating that with the art and narrative ideas suggests a short technological confinement/escape, one representative medieval realm with generated approaches and meaningful upgrades, and a brief technological arrival where the player uses a chosen magical reward. The technological visual kit can serve both short sections. This exact sequence remains a proposal; it does not commit to two complete biomes, a dragon or a city simulation.

The beta must include capable base combat, at least two obtainable build approaches, a wanted-attachment pursuit, an unexpected useful alternative, a boss and a satisfying saved payoff. Include a difficult-to-earn component whose combination can produce a conspicuous power spike, acquired through play rather than only a developer loadout. Several ordinary and awkward generated arrangements should work. Show the transfer decision and its consequence in real combat; a portal cinematic or selection screen cannot establish that it is enjoyable.

Internal experiments first resolve weapon/guard feel, contrasting combinations, assembled-room navigation and save/transfer behavior. Those rough experiments are not the owner beta. Independent packaged testing must cover input/focus, navigation, reward compatibility, reaction limits, save interruption and crossing recovery. Store generation/content versions and claimed state so an interrupted transition restores one coherent side without duplicating or losing earned rewards.

The decisive owner observations are whether a new ability changes a tactical choice, whether the next reward makes another choice interesting, whether the boss feels earned, whether a crossing feels exciting, and whether the next world invites another expedition. The strongest shared warning from the reviewers is that one safe attack/guard routine could remain optimal through all the scenery changes. Resolve that before increasing the world or attachment count.

No final title, ending, art treatment, transfer allowance, campaign length, content total or build schedule is selected here. The owner-selected first-person combined weapon/shield and the real-world dream premise are the foundation for further design and an implementation brief.
