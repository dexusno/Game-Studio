# Weapon/shield attachment catalogue

2026-09-08. Original creative proposals for the first-person cyborg/worlds concept. **120 component and enhancement ideas: 100 behavior or rule concepts, plus 20 supporting enhancement types.** None are implemented or validated through play. This is a pool for design and prototyping, not a promised release inventory or a final count of distinct abilities. Some concepts overlap and may merge after testing. Increasing an item's rank does not make it a new ability.

Klaus wants the weapon/shield to be the central evolving object: extensive technology, magic, attachments and possibly crystals/jewelry, with creative combinations, earned exceptional power and worthwhile rewards. Stackable upgrades are now an explicit design interest. First person and freely chosen attack/defense remain selected. The [equipment design](weapon-shield-design.md) defines the underlying combat and reward principles; the [worlds proposal](cyborg-worlds-design.md) holds the story and carryover discussion.

## How this becomes a build

Keep a large collection of discoveries and a readable active build. Most components change existing attacks, guard, movement, placement or a deliberate follow-up; each item should not demand its own button. Equipment capacity and compatibility create choices, while reward milestones can expand mounting or fusion options. Exact slots and capacity remain open.

These six catalogue groups describe functions, not six mandatory sockets or mutually exclusive lore materials. A crystal can hold elemental behavior, a technological attachment can deliver it, and a rune or piece of jewelry can connect it to another action. Some listed concepts compete for the same role; others need an explicit interface before they can work together. Do not promise universal compatibility.

Make the object visibly accumulate its history: an articulated technological mount, a cracked crystal held in a crafted collar, a charm attached to the weapon spine, a new edge on a shield plate. Put functional jewelry on the weapon or cyborg where its role remains legible. Calling the weapon the hero does not by itself decide that it is sentient.

## The catalogue

Descriptions are sketches of player-facing behavior. Words such as “eligible,” “compatible” and “supported” mark a rule that must be specified and communicated in a prototype; they are not permission for unexplained exceptions. Targets, resource types, placement, triggers and repetitions need an inspectable rule before implementation.

## Stacks, levels and evolution

**Use three different kinds of stacking and show which applies.**

| Kind | Example | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity | Extra ricochets, captured projectiles or echo repeats | Copies increase a disclosed quantity, with an item-specific maximum. Display the current total and what the next copy adds. |
| Supporting strength or rate | More chill per hit, greater impact, faster charging | Combine using one stated rule for that property. Do not silently multiply every bonus or count each percentage tier as a different ability. |
| Evolution | A familiar mechanism gains a new behavior at a milestone | Offer a meaningful branch that changes use. Further copies can support that branch or provide an alternative reward once completed. |

Illustrative Ricochet Rail progression: the first rank permits one deliberate bounce; another rank adds bounces; a later milestone offers **split on impact** or **converge on a marked target after a bounce**. These ranks and thresholds are examples, not tuned values. The convergence branch would supply its own marking interaction rather than secretly requiring a second item. Its split branch must state how it combines with Forking Prism, avoiding duplicate rewards that merely repeat an existing rule.

Unique rule-changing items do not blindly apply an entire passive rule twice. Their duplicates can feed a disclosed evolution or an alternative chosen improvement. Reaching an item's cap should not turn a difficult boss reward into worthless loot. Permanent pattern ownership, permanent mastery/evolution unlocks and temporary expedition stacks must have separate, readable states.

A weapon can grow through a campaign by acquiring new forms, mounting options, learned fusion patterns and mastery branches. Such milestones should follow meaningful exploration or challenges, with opportunities to practice. Do not make the player repeatedly grind basic damage merely to restore comfortable controls or baseline viability.

Some combinations should multiply each other's payoff. Extra projectiles can apply more frost; a well-timed impact can turn the resulting formation into a shatter cascade. Do not require every layer to remain a small additive bonus. Bound repeated processing and track consumed states so the same projectile, reward or spent charge cannot be credited forever. Compatible secondary effects may still trigger other effects, and a legitimate resource-positive loop or boss-melting build is not automatically a bug.

Show an item's stacking rule, maximum, prerequisites and whether the acquired improvement persists. Exact equipped capacity, rank totals, fusion costs and cross-world allowances remain design questions. In particular, a two-item transfer setting is not settled; preserving an exciting build foundation matters more than defending that provisional number.

## Combinations to explore

These are examples of compatible relationships, not hidden named recipes the game recognizes. Slot availability and proposed evolutions must be resolved in the actual loadout.

| Combination | Player setup and possible payoff | What can make it weak or strong |
|---|---|---|
| Forking Prism + Frost Heart + Ram Edge + Stormfracture | Spread frost through a formation, commit to an impact and set off a room-clearing shatter/lightning reaction. Fork or buildup stacks can accelerate the setup. | Requires grouped, sufficiently chilled targets and a useful impact; Stormfracture creates lightning itself, without requiring a second elemental core. |
| Mirror Facet + Siege Capacitor + Echo Chamber | Catch a dangerous volley, aim the stored barrage and repeat the counterattack after a delay. Capture and echo stacks expand the spectacle. | Catching and aiming are active skills; a lone enemy with no capturable attack supplies less fuel. A manually released counterattack is an attack for Echo. |
| Anchor Foot + Gate Lens + Ember Heart + Pact of Ash | Place a panel, create a burning approach and feed attacks through the panel into an elemental firing lane. Burning ground restores the panel through the pact. | Depends on positioning and active firing; the panel does not generate its own attacks. Repair must not repeatedly claim one expired burning event. |
| Tether Bolt + Gravity Heart + Impact Force | Pull a susceptible target into a compressed group, then use a deliberate impact to fling the formation through a hazardous lane. | Pulling and knocking back at the same time can scatter the setup; sequencing can make those same components work together. Requires an available baseline or equipped impact action. |
| Heat Exchanger + Overclock Bus + Recovery Flux | With a heat-generating weapon action, overclocking spends shield reserve and produces heat; the exchanger recovers shield resource from that heat. Recovery stacks increase a qualifying gain. | The heat-generating action is an explicit prerequisite. Without one, the exchanger has no input. A resource-positive result can be an earned payoff if every gain corresponds to an actual event. |
| Twin Sigil + Needle Channel + Weakpoint Focus | Deliberately link two targets, line up a piercing precision attack and use the shared pulse to reach the second threat. | Pairing and aim matter. The shared pulse's inherited effects must be declared; a precision bonus must not become a secret automatic critical hit on every linked target. |

A combination can be situational without being a trap. On-kill recovery may excel in a crowded encounter and contribute little against a lone guardian. A launch effect can ruin close-range follow-ups until the player adds a tether or changes timing. Heat conversion with no heat source is an unmet prerequisite, which equipment inspection must reveal. A player should be able to understand why a build underperformed and make a deliberate improvement.

## Rewards and experimentation

Keep the three questions separate: **what changes, how do I obtain it, and what stays mine?** Difficult boss, elite and exploration rewards can reveal distinctive new behaviors and permanent patterns. Duplicates can make an existing favorite evolve. Supporting stacks can turn a good engine into a spectacular one. New choices also need usable ordinary components and a satisfying starting weapon; all interesting play cannot wait for rare drops.

World cultures give the components identity and acquisition context, rather than isolating them into non-interacting systems. A medieval crystal and a cyberpunk focusing assembly should have an understandable mechanical relationship. New-world discoveries can extend imported equipment. Preserve learned ownership and test whether crossing rules retain enough of a successful build to make the reward feel valuable.

Offer a quick safe way to try an installation, a clear comparison when replacing it, and upcoming combat in which its effect can matter. Some enemies and bosses should visibly use related abilities before dropping their adaptation. Secrets should reward observation, exploration and suitable tools, with learnable clues. A known desired component needs a reasonable pursuit instead of an ever-growing undirected loot lottery.

## What the first beta needs to prove

Choose a coherent subset from this catalogue; the catalogue is not the first-beta checklist. The representative beta should demonstrate two contrasting obtainable builds, a useful duplicate stack, a behavioral evolution, a hard-earned combination with a conspicuous power payoff, and meaningful use of an earned ability after crossing worlds. Exact inventory size remains open. Build the reward sequence so these tests can actually occur during an owner session.

Test the base weapon's feel, the clarity and usefulness of new components, the pleasure of stacking a favorite, meaningful installation choices, and the urge to try another build. Verify effect accounting, save ownership, reaction termination and performance at extreme combinations. A large number of generated loadouts is not evidence of a large number of enjoyable playstyles.

Some entries carry major additional production work: grappling, gliding, riding rails, dynamic stone forms, grasping enemy limbs, moving hazards, revival states and cross-world spell storage all affect movement, collision, navigation, animation or saves. They are possibilities to evaluate, not assumed cheap features. Grouping them into a short description does not make them implemented.

Independent designer input supplied the shield/capture/movement family and reviewed stacking and count claims. That review identified duplicate resource credit and endless interception/refund triggers as failure modes. This catalogue remains original design work without gameplay validation; no new runtime, assets or external research were produced for it.
