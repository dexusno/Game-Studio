# Gameplay directions after the Sixfold Recoil playtest

Research date: 2026-09-07. Research and proposals only; no replacement game selected, implemented, purchased or downloaded by this task. Owner: bounded gameplay research agent; the producer owns studio/game records and the engine/asset investigation.

Klaus played Sixfold Recoil `0.1.0-beta`, fingerprint `467e2a4ca6c3`, and found it boring: too little challenge or strategy, one repeated attack/recall rhythm, no powerups or progression, and presentation resembling the earlier Breakout game. This is direct owner feedback about that build. The technical tests recorded in `games/scrapstorm/QA.md` did not predict enjoyment. The existing hook and historical design remain preserved; the proposals below materially change the promise and go through the producer.

The new brief is substantially greater ambition: learnable skill, interacting combat and build systems, exploration, meaningful progress and strong 3D presentation in Unreal. Klaus clarified that “ten times” was an expression of scale, not a multiplier, mechanic quota or promise of more fun. There is no hard development-day or owner-hour cap. New cash expenditure remains unallocated. Parent investigation confirms local Unreal 5.8.2, RTX 4090 and approximately 63 GiB RAM; this is environment evidence supplied by the producer, not a performance result for these unbuilt games.

## What has to change in the next owner playtest

The representative slice should let the player make decisions at three connected scales: **how to win this exchange, what build to develop, and what objective or route to pursue next**. The player must experience those scales in the build they are asked to judge. A single room with one attack followed by a promise of future upgrades would repeat the failed experiment.

Useful acceptance behavior, common to all three proposals:

- The initial kit supports an aimed attack, a distinct pressure or control action, active defense/movement and a resource tradeoff. These are available early enough to judge; basic expression is not locked behind repetitive failure.
- A mixed encounter rewards at least two viable solutions. Range, enemy priority, positioning and action timing matter; always circling the perimeter while repeating the same input sequence is challenged by enemy combinations and terrain.
- The first substantial reward changes a tactic the player can use immediately. A later choice can reinforce that tactic or give up part of it for another advantage. Descriptions preview that behavior clearly.
- The biome contains a route choice, optional risk, a discoverable reward and an enemy/boss that tests the developing build. A door that selects the next identical combat room does not suffice.
- Returning to the hub produces a real unlock or persistent world change, and re-entry can demonstrate its effect. Saves, death consequences and difficulty settings work. The slice's unlock is real, though the rest of the campaign remains unbuilt.
- One environment and the important combatants have a coherent visual direction in motion: modeled silhouettes, authored materials, lighting, animation, hit reactions, readable effects and a deliberate sound palette. A stationary screenshot or an engine feature switch cannot pass this criterion.

These are observable design targets, not proof of fun. An exploratory owner session can cover a first biome and the start of a different build; there is no artificial minimum run length. Record what Klaus chooses and why, where he becomes bored/confused, whether practice improves a result, and whether he voluntarily wants to continue after seeing a reward or finishing a boss.

## Ranked original proposals

Names below are descriptive research labels, not proposed cleared product names. Ranking is based on Klaus's stated preference for richer, challenging, visually stronger 3D games. It does not automatically select a game or score financial prospects.

### 1. First-person reactor raider

**Promise:** explore a storm-powered industrial ruin, fight through dangerous multi-level spaces, and configure how your suit turns combat-generated energy into weapons, movement and control. The signature choice is the *use* of a shared charge reserve, not throwing and recovering a conserved projectile.

**Concrete exchange.** Move and aim freely, fire the selected gun, swap between carried weapons, dash, throw a control device, and discharge the suit. Precision hits and deliberate close-range finishes build charge. A player can spend it on a short shield pulse to cross an exposed lane, a burst that interrupts a heavy attacker, or an empowered weapon shot. Spending charge leaves less available for the next emergency. There is no mandatory “shoot, charge, discharge” sequence: ordinary gunplay remains useful and defense is available on an independent recovery, with charge buying a stronger version.

A shield carrier protects a rifle unit while a flanker closes from a side stair. The player can reposition above the shield, freeze the flanker and preserve the reserve, or spend the reserve to stagger the carrier and rush its exposed ally. A sniper keeps static cover play unsafe. A visible breaker on a power conduit offers an environmental alternative, but hitting it at the wrong time can electrify the player's crossing. Success means an intentional response to the formation; failure should be attributable to aim, movement, resource commitment or target order, rather than invisible stat requirements.

**Build and progression.** The suit has a generator rule and a consumer rule selected at safe stations. A precision generator paired with a piercing discharge rewards controlled weak-point shots and line formation. A close-combat generator paired with a protective discharge rewards aggressive entries, enemy separation and timed retreats. A run upgrade may make the control device conductive, letting a carefully placed field relay an empowered shot; an alternative extends defense at the cost of maximum offensive charge. Modules need explicit caps and trigger rules so a feedback loop cannot automatically solve combat. Weapons differ through recoil, effective distance, projectile behavior and alternate use, not rarity alone.

At a map junction, choose the cooling district to obtain a control module or the exposed turbine route for a weapon workshop. Optional machinery repairs open shortcuts and future expedition entrances. Extracted research unlocks new starting module options, weapon plans and access to the next region. Run modules and temporary bonuses reset; learned plans and region access persist. A completed main expedition should later advance a larger campaign objective, with additional challenge contracts after its ending; total game length remains a separate production decision.

**Representative first biome.** One inhabited storm foundry with connected interior/exterior spaces, a route fork that reconnects, a vertical optional workshop and an extraction/hub transition. The kit needs a precision weapon, a close-range option and an area weapon available through play, plus the control device and suit choices. Enemies must include pressure from flanking, protected ranged fire, suppression, area denial and a threatening elite. The turbine guardian combines these lessons with moving safe positions and exposed components. Rewards must support both example builds before the boss, and the hub must save a module or access unlock that can be used on re-entry. These functions define representativeness; exact counts and tuning belong to the implementation brief.

**Visual direction and camera.** Weathered painted steel, storm light, huge rotating machinery and warm maintenance interiors. Use physical mesh detail where the first-person camera approaches it, intentional material contrast and restrained electrical effects; no tiny toy figures or flat icon-shaped enemies. First-person makes vertical weak points and machinery scale matter. It needs excellent hands/weapon animation, recoil, camera comfort and spatial audio; reducing full-body hero exposure does not eliminate animation work. Optional camera bob/shake and a stable horizon matter for comfort.

**Main burden / exclusions.** High-quality shooting, locomotion, first-person weapon sets, varied enemies that navigate elevation, reliable collision and safe procedural assembly if introduced later. The engineer must prove module interaction rules and combat readability before expanding the reward pool. Exclude multiplayer, wall-running/grappling until they earn a specific encounter purpose, arbitrary destructibility, hundreds of guns, a survival-crafting inventory and automatically generated open worlds. Authored routes come first.

**Strongest counterargument.** This is a crowded FPS-roguelite space, and a shared energy system can collapse into another repeated optimal rotation. Battle Shapers already occupies part of the fantasy. Our difference depends on reserve allocation and route-dependent build choices producing distinct player behavior, not on naming a system “reactor.”

**Cheapest disproof / next test.** Internally implement one mixed, vertical combat space with the two sample module pairings, two guns and the control device; offer the same enemies and objective to both. Stop broadening if both builds converge on identical movement and firing, if the reserve only creates waiting, or if raw shooting does not feel worth practicing. This inexpensive technical/feel rig is preparation for the representative biome, not the promised owner beta. Owner question for that biome: **“Which choice changed your plan, and what would you build differently on your next expedition?”**

### 2. Third-person monster hunter with combat-earned grafts

**Promise:** hunt through a ruined mountain sanctuary, decide which parts of dangerous creatures to disable or preserve, and use harvested powers to change your fighting style during the expedition. The player reads bodies and openings, not only projectiles.

**Concrete exchange.** Over-the-shoulder camera with free look and optional target lock. Use directional movement, light and committed heavy attacks, guard/perfect parry, dodge, an aimed binding stake and two equipped graft abilities. The starting spear controls distance; the hammer trades reach and recovery for staggering force. Guard absorbs limited pressure, while a correctly timed parry exposes an attack opportunity. Dodging avoids danger but loses position and offensive opportunity. No endless invulnerability chain.

A plated beast has a charging limb and a toxin sac. Breaking the limb suppresses its charge, making the immediate encounter safer. Piercing the sac vents a hazardous cloud that can kill its supporting creatures but destroys the gland needed for a desirable graft. Keeping the sac intact means controlling the space and defeating the beast more carefully. A binding stake briefly holds a limb after a readable opening; it does not immobilize every boss on demand. A human-sized spear enemy punishes blindly committing to the beast while a ranged support unit is still alive. Reliable animation cues, directional reach and recovery windows make improvement learnable.

**Build and progression.** A preserved gland can become a short poison cone; a broken shell can become a timed counterguard. Slots force a choice between active abilities and modifiers: the cone can spread a mark that a spear thrust detonates, while a counterguard modifier releases stored stagger through a heavy attack. Choosing one costs access to another defense or burst. Rewards change spacing and enemy priority. Not every body part yields a unique power; a small set of authored harvest outcomes must cover meaningful tactics.

The region offers alternative hunts and a sanctuary route. Tracking evidence reveals which quarry carries the ingredient your build wants, making exploration purposeful. Banked recipes, weapon disciplines, sanctuary functions and opened routes survive failure; a run's temporary graft combination and unbanked harvest do not. Discovering a new quarry and learning how to harvest it progresses the campaign. Permanent power is bounded enough that the starting kit can win through skill, with optional explicit difficulty tiers after a regional victory.

**Representative first biome.** A connected mountain sanctuary with elevation, sightline changes, a safe preparation point, an optional hunt, a shortcut and a major guardian. It must include both weapon disciplines, the stake, multiple distinct graft choices, at least two actual harvest tradeoffs, enemies that require different timing responses, and one mixed fight that punishes tunnel vision. The guardian changes its available attacks after selected part breaks. Return to the sanctuary, save a recipe, and start another hunt with a changed starting option. A single boss with a bare sword would not represent the proposal.

**Visual direction and camera.** Worn leather and bronze, huge mineral growths, mist between terraces and animals with readable anatomical silhouettes. The hero's graft changes should visibly affect gear or limbs. Third-person shows weapon reach, dodge direction, part changes and acquired equipment; it also exposes all blend errors. Keep the camera pulled out during large attacks, let the player see elevated threats, and prevent nearby scenery from hiding a duel. Fully 3D traversal supports stairs, ledges and small climbs; free climbing is excluded initially.

**Main burden / exclusions.** This has the highest animation burden: retargeting alone will not supply believable impacts, creature tells, locomotion transitions, hit reactions or part-break state changes. The engineer must agree on combat targeting, part hitboxes, interrupt rules and animation windows before assets are multiplied. Exclude dozens of monster skeletons, procedural anatomical destruction, mount riding, multiplayer, a huge crafting inventory and MMO-style hunting grinds. A modest number of sharply different, deeply authored creatures is the content strategy.

**Strongest counterargument.** Animation and camera weaknesses can make a rich design feel worse than a simpler polished game. Arboria already proves that mutations plus third-person roguelite combat is an existing combination; our distinguishing bet is the conflict between disabling a threat now and preserving its reward. It could feel like tedious ingredient farming if immediate combat changes are weak.

**Cheapest disproof / next test.** One creature with two authored break states, one interfering support enemy, both weapon disciplines and one usable graft reward. Reject the harvest premise if the optimal target is always obvious, preserving a part only lengthens health depletion, or camera/animation clarity cannot support fair timing. Before the owner evaluates the game's larger promise, place this in the representative biome with progression and coherent art. Owner question: **“Did you deliberately fight the creature differently because of what you wanted from it?”**

### 3. Third-person breach engineer in a changing ruin

**Promise:** raid a partially powered alien structure, turn its machinery into combat advantages, and choose whether to spend scarce power opening a deeper route or securing your escape. This is an exploration and tactical-action roguelite, with power allocation changing both the fight and the level.

**Concrete exchange.** Move, aim and fire an individual ranged weapon; swap to a close-range tool, dodge, deploy a field anchor, and activate a selected device. An anchor can pull light enemies across a firing lane or briefly pin an exposed heavy. Its field has a visible boundary, a limited duration and a recharge. It cannot disable everything. A shield emitter can instead cover a retreat; equipping it gives up another active device. Shots at exposed armor joints disable a chosen attack, while the cutting tool rewards entering a dangerous opening.

The player reaches a freight hall with mobile cover, a high firing platform and dormant lifts. Routing one recovered cell to the lift opens a flank and traversal reward but leaves no cell for the defensive turret. Routing it to the turret makes the firefight safer while keeping the upper path shut. A jammer can disable deployed equipment until interrupted, and a pursuer pressures stationary engineering play. The player can win through movement and precise shots without spending the cell, but it is harder. An anchor field can drag a pursuer into a warned machinery hazard; arbitrary physics accidents must not dominate consistent combat rules.

**Build and progression.** Upgrade anchors into short high-force bursts or longer control zones; pair armor-breaking shots with a device that gains charge from disabled components. Alternatively, build around mobile cover and close-range weapon bursts. Recovered modules compete for limited powered slots, so a powerful utility device has an offensive cost. Combat batteries recharge through controlled actions; expedition power cells are separately named, visibly different, scarce route resources. Separating the two prevents ordinary fighting from consuming the item needed to avoid a progression lock.

Exploration offers information and power as well as loot. A restored map relay reveals a valuable side site; an optional reactor theft triggers a signposted security response and changes the return route. Extraction banks research and restores functions to a persistent workshop. A successful major raid opens a deeper district; defeat loses unbanked materials but preserves discovered plans and completed access objectives. The next attempt can approach a different functional layout. No arbitrary global timer is required: chosen interventions escalate the local opposition.

**Representative first biome.** One flooded freight district with two functional routes, an optional power objective, a machinery encounter, a workshop/vendor decision and a security construct finale. It requires a meaningful rifle/close-range choice, alternative deployable builds, a jammer, a mobile pursuer, a high-angle threat and an elite that reacts to disabled components. An extraction decision, readable security escalation, a saved workshop unlock and re-entry through a changed route must work. Variation can initially use authored configurations of power, patrols and rewards; procedural terrain is unnecessary to test the promise.

**Visual direction and camera.** Dense stone/metal structures, shallow water, plant intrusion, local pools of power and a visibly equipped exploration suit. Third-person shows the anchor relation to the player, moving cover, gear and traversal; shoulder aiming brings close-up material quality into view. Sightline-aware camera collision and clear offscreen threat cues are essential. Distinct powered/unpowered machinery states should be readable through shape, motion and sound as well as color. The environment must be interesting to navigate while effects stay controlled.

**Main burden / exclusions.** Authored interactable machinery, AI reacting to power/route changes, navigation updates, camera obstruction, saveable world state and systemic bug combinations. The engineer must agree on a bounded vocabulary of actors and state transitions. Simulate only tagged machinery and controlled movable objects; exclude arbitrary building destruction, a full electricity simulator, universal object manipulation, survival meters, settlement construction, companion AI and online extraction. Those cuts protect the intended tactical choices rather than shrinking it back to an arena loop.

**Strongest counterargument.** This can turn into slow key collection with shooting between switches. It also has a greater interaction/level-authoring burden than its asset count suggests. The machinery must offer alternative solutions and consequences; mandatory switches disguised as choices would fail the promise.

**Cheapest disproof / next test.** One freight encounter with both power routes, a jammer/pursuer combination, an anchor and a fixed reward. Test different allocations against the same objective. Reject the systemic premise if one option simply dominates, interactions are needed only for doors, or the player ignores them because gunplay is equally safe. Owner question for the full biome: **“Did you make this place work for your plan, or did you just follow its switches?”**

## Recommendation and engineering handoff

Recommend developing a reviewable brief for the **first-person reactor raider**, with the third-person monster hunter as the strongest alternative if seeing the character and deliberate melee are more important to Klaus than precision gunplay. The first option most directly tests the requested combination of challenge, skill, builds, exploration and strong spatial presentation. It is not recommended because it is the smallest option: its distinctive reserve decisions and route-dependent builds still need to earn interest against strong competitors. The breach engineer ranks third because its systemic exploration promise is attractive but easier to dilute into busywork and harder to author reliably.

The producer has been consulted on camera, installed engine/hardware and broad engineering exposure. No gameplay engineer has yet proved these specific systems. Before creating a selected implementation plan, the engineering handoff must settle movement/camera feasibility, initial animation sources, enemy navigation on the representative geometry, the bounded ability/status interaction model, save boundaries and the actual art/asset pipeline. Detailed frame timings, budgets and final content totals require that investigation, not invented estimates here.

The next iteration is a concrete design/engineering/art decision on one direction, followed by its internal disproof rig and then a representative biome if the premise survives. Preserve the old prototype as a failed owner-fit experiment. Do not treat a successful compile, a dramatic Unreal screenshot or this shortlist as evidence that the new game is fun.
