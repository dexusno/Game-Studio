# Scrapstorm: independent commercial review

**2026-09-07, Europe/Oslo. Reviewer: commercial analyst. Corrected individual assessment after the peer challenge and Klaus's production-framing correction.** This evaluates the complete authoritative v0.1 handoff: all 17 sections, sample round, unresolved decisions, prototype criteria and source caveats. The initial assessment was written before reading the gameplay report; the exchange and changes are preserved below. No game was played or built. Findings about Scrapstorm's behaviour are design hypotheses; store inventories and prices are observations of advertised products, not hands-on verification.

**Corrected verdict: proceed to a focused playable test, with a profitability case developed alongside it.** The current brief contains a plausible sellable action game; its unresolved question is whether spending visible protection to attack and recovering it is enjoyable enough to sustain the paid package. The small paper-rule fixes belong in that recommended experiment, rather than creating another preliminary gate. **USD 4.99 remains an unvalidated price hypothesis.** The current inventory does not support USD 6.99–9.99, but a worthwhile revised package could justify reconsideration within Klaus's stated price ceiling. Additional useful enemies, upgrades or encounters are legitimate options if they improve player value and plausible profit. More content and a lower price are neither automatic remedies nor prohibited options. Confidence in the eventual commercial outcome remains low; confidence that the concept merits this focused test is medium.

**Correction, September 7:** I previously treated the brief's 6–7 implementation days and 4–6 owner hours as practical caps and used human development durations to challenge AI production feasibility. That was the wrong frame. Klaus has set no hard development-time limit and reports that this studio built Breakout in a couple of hours in an evening. The coordinator checked current Breakout documentation describing a substantial native Windows game and recorded validation; that supports AI delivery capability, without claiming every current feature existed in the first evening or that this review reran its tests. The earlier figures are planning hypotheses only. The objective is an **enjoyable and plausibly profitable game**, not a fast finish or an arbitrary copy count. The corrected assessment below supersedes the old schedule-based reasoning.

Our working title needs replacement before public-facing branding. This is an observed search collision, not a legal determination. It does not invalidate the proposed robot game.

## What would make people care?

- **Buy:** a clear ten-second sequence in which six attractive objects visibly leave a vulnerable little robot, strike, and return. That is more concrete than another promise of many upgrades. The miniature workshop could give an otherwise abstract weapon system personality.
- **Enjoy:** accurate hits, readable damage, strong collection sound, useful positioning and rapid recovery from mistakes. The no-cleanup transition, no permanent stat grind, deterministic shielding and quick restart are sensible choices. These are likely strengths, not observed fun.
- **Replay:** becoming better at choosing a throw and arranging a return. The brief is substantially more credible as a short mastery game than as a build-discovery game. Two choices from six definitions do not establish many hours of discovery.
- **Recommend:** a recovery that another player can understand and want to reproduce. A failed throw followed by a deliberate rescue is a more persuasive clip than orbiting junk or a results screen.
- **Skip:** a six-minute loop that is solved by retreating, firing and holding recall; unclear tiny projectiles; a promise of roguelite depth that disappears after a few clears; or an attractive robot whose actual package looks like a paid prototype.

The primary audience hypothesis should be **players seeking active aiming and short arcade mastery**, with light build variation. Existing arena-roguelite players are an adjacent audience, not an automatically reachable pool. Toy art is not inherently incompatible with action: the current successful price incumbent is itself a potato shooter. Conversely, GNOG, Wilmot's Warehouse and WALL-E are art/fiction references, not evidence that their audiences want this combat. Show danger and aiming in the first media; do not imply crafting, physical construction or a relaxing upcycling game when the six objects have uniform mechanics. This addresses sections 1–3, 6, 9–10 and 15.

## What buyer criticism actually says

I inspected **25 reviews across five games**, requesting up to the three most recent English Steam-purchase reviews per polarity, separately. Boomerang Battles had no qualifying negatives; Rocket Fist returned one. Low-information and unrelated entries were retained in the sampling record but excluded from theme interpretation. This deliberately overrepresents negative feedback and cannot estimate prevalence. Review dates span 2017–2026; historical complaints are not verified present bugs. Full links, dates, filters and paraphrases are in the [review sample](commercial-evidence/review-sample.md).

- **Akane:** a [May 2026 negative reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199792789017/recommended/884260/) reports repetitive patterns, weak equipment changes and frustrating repeated boss presentation. An [August 2026 positive reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198071556249/recommended/884260/) finds the challenge fair and strongly replayable, but explicitly reports a **GBP 1.99 sale purchase**. Both the mastery opportunity and the disagreement matter; neither establishes full-price willingness to pay for Scrapstorm.
- **SNKRX:** [one positive reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198795817109/recommended/915310/) values finally succeeding with an extreme build; [one negative reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198041552270/recommended/915310/) criticizes obscure interactions, movement and the damage-loss spiral. Build strength can be enjoyable, but unclear rules and constrained evasion can spoil it.
- **Spellbound Survivors:** [a July 2025 recommender](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198128506170/recommended/2602450/) prefers the first ten minutes to the repetitive later twenty; [another](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198027483587/recommended/2602450/) calls the inventory thin versus its reference game. A [January 2024 negative review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197996972442/recommended/2602450/) reports progression shifting from frustratingly weak to passive play. These challenge filler and dominant strategies without implying that permanent grind would help Scrapstorm.
- **Rocket Fist:** its [2017 negative reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198072187205/recommended/413500/) reports collision and aim confusion in a closely related one-shot/recover setup. A [2020 positive reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198097035260/recommended/413500/) praises simplicity and couch suitability. The latter audience advantage does not transfer to a solo game.
- **Boomerang Battles:** a [2023 positive reviewer](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198038288899/recommended/2337660/) likes the cute boomerang premise while warning of difficulty and repeated starts. Cute art and hard action can coexist, but three short positive reports do not demonstrate durable retention.

## Ranked failure modes and concrete changes

These are my product judgments from the whole brief, not claims of reproduced bugs.

|---|---|---|
| **1. Central interaction does not earn repetition** | **4–6, 11, 17:** global full-speed recall may make attack → recall the universal sequence. Since the player outruns Biters and the tray has no geometry, retreating can be both safe and effective. Partial volleys need meaningful arrival gaps; the story invents useful two-piece windows without demonstrating them. | Keep global recall initially, but test deliberate partial shots against routine cycling and perimeter kiting. Observe threat-sensitive choices and improving aim/timing. A consistent rhythm is acceptable if players enjoy mastering it and voluntarily replay after clears; every volley need not be novel. Use unwanted repetition and stalled learning to target the next mechanical change. Hold/redesign when the evidence offers no credible route to enjoyable repeat play or an honest alternative product. |
| **2. The 14-pair figure is simply `6 choose 2 minus the incompatible Lance/Scatter pair`. Offer restrictions and order affect availability and timing; arithmetic is not replay evidence. The [coordinator's paper audit](coordinator-evidence/audit.md) also found that cutting to four definitions can break the second two-card offer: in a Lance/Scatter/Return/Coil pool, first show Lance + Return and pick Lance; Return is excluded as previously shown and Scatter as incompatible, leaving only Coil. A four-card cut must remove the incompatible pair or allow a previously declined card to return. This is a necessary offer-rule correction, not extra content.

**Keep in the first test:** one robot/arena, conserved pieces, contrasting threats, clear event feedback, finite encounters, instant retry and no permanent power grind. This isolates the interaction; it does not freeze the shipping inventory. **Strong candidates for a revised product:** a new enemy role that changes timing or positioning, genuinely different arena geometry, and more upgrades that change technique rather than only percentages. Bank Rims is one existing reserve worth comparing if bank shots create useful choices. Comparable record conditions or leaderboards could support mastery if players value them and their actual implementation/support cost is justified. **Cut or defer:** repeated encounters, cosmetic differences or complicated material simulation that add cost without enjoyment, clarity or saleable identity. An additional arena or larger build pool is not itself a reason to hold the game; quantity alone is not a reason to add it either. These are recommendations, not changes to the source brief or authorization to implement them.

## Price, cost and visibility

**USD 4.99 is possible, not established.** Akane provides the most relevant small-arena package comparison, while SNKRX and Brotato make a shallow build inventory difficult to sell on breadth. I would lead with a distinctive arcade action pleasure and an honest short-run promise. For this exact brief, the convincing route is earned repeat mastery. A deliberately tiny one-clear product could exist in principle, but its value would require a separate authored-experience proposition and test; the current story beats do not provide evidence for it. Price-test the exact inventory and expected experience, not the exciting future potential. My initial draft's USD 2.99 comparison cell was anchored to a cheaper incumbent, not to evidence of Scrapstorm's value, and is withdrawn as an implied recommended range.

A short game can be finished within Steam's ordinary [two-week/under-two-hour refund conditions](https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/). That creates exposure, **not a measured refund forecast or a reason to pad beyond two hours**. Trustworthy scope and perceived value are the useful response.

**What would make this plausibly profitable?** Let `C` be total incremental cash cost, including release, additional AI/tool charges, assets and expected support costs. Let `q` be the average contribution from a paid copy after actual regional/discounted pricing, refunds, store-collected taxes, platform share and variable costs. Cash break-even is `ceil(C / q)`. Track owner effort beside that result; cash profit alone need not make a project worthwhile. Gross sticker-price multiplication is not this calculation.

## Cheapest evidence that could change my verdict

**Recommendation only; no implementation or recruitment is authorized by this review.** Start with a focused combat experiment targeting roughly three minutes of play, two enemies, no cards or fiction expansion, distinct throw/block/hull/recall sounds and rapid restart. Its small first slice isolates the interaction; neither its development duration nor the shipping content is capped by it. Incorporate the paper-rule fixes into that experiment and record actual marginal AI/tool cost alongside these observations:

1. Test harmless and damaging returns with order counterbalanced where possible. Record whether movement deliberately creates new return crossings, rather than overlapping contact damage, and whether improved results simply reflect more damage. Show neither as the preferred version. Keep harmless returns if their execution rhythm earns mastery and replay; damaging returns are not a requirement.
2. Before normal sessions, deliberately attempt hold-recall tanking, perimeter kiting and close-range fire/recall cycling. These are design probes, not automatic reasons to add enemies or movement penalties.
3. Observe partial-shot opportunities: held count when a player fires, why they stopped recall, and whether they notice the defence cost. A low held count alone is not evidence of a deliberate choice.
4. Retain the brief's five-person directional criteria, but distinguish **replay after a successful clear from retry after defeat**. Add one second voluntary session or several completed attempts for available players before claiming repeat value. A first retry can be curiosity or a desire to understand the controls. Record reasons for stopping as seriously as retries.
5. Only after enjoyable repeat play and a representative finished-direction encounter, show an honest package card: one arena, four encounters, two ordinary enemies plus finale, two upgrade choices, local records, USD 4.99 hypothesis. Ask what they would choose instead and what makes the package worth buying. Distinguish objection to price from lack of interest in the experience itself. A lower-price test cannot rescue a failed loop; any substantially smaller one-clear proposition needs an explicit revision first. Stated intent remains weaker than paid behaviour; this is not a conversion forecast.

If one recovery supplies the enjoyable action, promote and test it. If the base rhythm works but two enemy types or the initial card pool run out of interesting play, test worthwhile additional content and assess its actual incremental cost. Continue useful iteration while there is a concrete route to better enjoyment and a credible positive economic case. **Hold or redesign if there is no convincing route to enjoyable play or to proceeds exceeding the relevant costs.** A successful combat experiment should lead to a representative paid-package test and a cost/demand assessment, keeping USD 4.99 conditional unless a revised package justifies another price.

## Rubric and independent challenge record
