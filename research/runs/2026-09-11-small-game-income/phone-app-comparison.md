# Small games versus phone apps

Follow-up comparison, accessed 2026-09-11. The owner asks how chances of profit compare with phone apps inspired by rising trends or interesting topics. The governing target remains NOK 20,000 cash profit per product, followed by several products only if results justify it. This is a question and research comparison, not authorization to begin an app or switch projects.

The analytical preference is **a narrow useful app with an identifiable paying audience first; a distinctive small offline game next; a novelty/topic app dependent on short-lived attention last**. This is a conditional judgment about selection strategy, not measured odds. No matched dataset compares first-time, few-day games and phone apps at this profit threshold, and no actual app niche has yet been validated. The earlier game recommendation was within the game comparison; it does not establish that games outperform useful software.

An app's potential advantage is that its benefit can be concrete: saving time, preventing repeated mistakes or organizing a specific activity. Customer workarounds, spending and unsolved complaints can be investigated before building. For a game, willingness to pay and enjoyment remain harder to establish without a representative playable. Both still need good execution and a practical way to reach customers.

| Strategy | Relative fit for this owner | Required evidence | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrow offline utility with a paid unlock | Strongest conditional commercial candidate | A recurring task, deficient alternatives, a justified price and reachable buyers | Too small a paying audience or a free tool already solves the problem |
| Small polished premium PC game | Strong alternative, particularly for limited ongoing operations | Appealing actual play, distinct presentation and credible discovery | Weak sales despite a finished game |
| Subscription app solving an ongoing need | Could yield recurring revenue; weaker fit for minimal ongoing work if service-heavy | Continued use, renewals, margin after acquisition and operating costs | Churn, support, backend costs and recurring reliability obligations |
| App primarily exploiting an interesting/trending topic | Weak without a useful job or an owned audience | Evidence of purchase intent beyond searches and social attention | Attention disappears before launch or people expect the content free |

RevenueCat's 2026 report analyzes over 115,000 apps, primarily 2025 performance. It reports roughly USD 72 median monthly revenue one year after launch, versus USD 2,574 at the top-decile threshold. This is a trailing monthly revenue observation, not profit or annual income. The sample uses RevenueCat subscription apps with active revenue and minimum install/revenue thresholds; it is not all app launches or a benchmark for paid offline utilities. These restrictions preclude a numerical success-rate comparison with the Steam cases. [RevenueCat methodology and results](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

A trend is useful when it produces a recurring job. Illustrative, unvalidated directions include a specialist hobby measurement/planning tool, a field log that produces a useful export, or a collector workflow with a specific missing function. These are not researched opportunities or claims of rising demand. A phone should contribute something useful, such as on-site access, camera input, reminders or offline use. A topic reference or generic chatbot alone does not establish willingness to pay. Apple's minimum-functionality rules also require adequate utility or lasting entertainment and generally exclude simple content aggregators. [Apple App Review Guidelines, section 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

## Per-product economics

Using the previous scenario's 80% price realization, 8% refunds, 20% blended included-tax assumption and NOK 2,000 direct costs, a 49 NOK game with a 30% platform share requires 1,046 purchases for NOK 20,000 cash profit. A hypothetical 99 NOK one-time utility with a 15% platform share requires 427. These are independent price assumptions, not measured willingness to pay. Apple's Small Business Program provides a 15% commission for eligible enrolled developers; the actual applicable program/terms must be checked. [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)

The game's contribution is 49 × .8 × .92 / 1.2 × .70 = 21.0373 NOK per attempted purchase. The utility's is 99 × .8 × .92 / 1.2 × .85 = 51.612 NOK. Both use ceil(22,000 / contribution). If the utility costs 40 NOK in acquisition per attempted purchase, contribution falls to 11.612 NOK and the threshold rises to 1,895 purchases. The acquisition cost is a sensitivity input, not a measured ad price; unpaid reach also consumes time. At equal selling prices and all other assumptions equal, the 15% share yields about 21% more contribution than a 30% share, not a transformative demand advantage.

No personal income tax or owner wage is included. The NOK 2,000 allowance is held equal solely to isolate price/platform effects, not a quote for publishing either product. Real phone-app costs can include developer memberships, test devices/build access, store setup, support and hosting. AI inference costs are relevant only if the shipped app calls a paid service. A subscription should be modeled through retained paid months and service costs, not by assuming every initial subscriber stays for twelve months.

## Work and decision

A simple on-device utility may require less art/content work than a game, but no few-day completion claim is established. Adding account systems, synchronization, live data or AI services changes that scope. Mobile operating-system and store changes can require later maintenance even for an offline app. Google Play's target-API policy can restrict submissions and discovery for outdated targets; Apple's removal review applies to apps both stale and minimally downloaded, not every app after a fixed interval. [Google policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16561298?hl=en-GB), [Apple App Store Improvements](https://developer.apple.com/support/app-store-improvements/)

Phone stores also have launch lead time. New personal Google Play accounts covered by the policy need a closed test with twelve opted-in testers for fourteen continuous days before applying for production access. This does not apply identically to every existing account. [Google testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB)

For maximum profit per owner hour, compare a small number of **concrete utility problems** with the proposed game before selecting production. Each candidate needs: evidence of the repeated problem, what people use/pay today, a reachable audience, a small complete solution, and acceptable costs/support. Only a utility meeting those conditions should outrank the game. For minimal operations, favor an offline paid unlock or paid game; consider subscriptions only when continuing value and retention justify them. No app has been selected, tested, built, bought, published or marketed in this follow-up.
