**Recommendation to the producer:** Parcel Paradox is the stronger of these two first experiments, because it can become a finite, understandable product. Its claimed originality is weaker after checking close competitors. ## Parcel Paradox

**Positioning hypothesis:** a cozy, finite packing puzzle about keeping living magical cargo happy during a delivery. In the first ten seconds, moving the warm dragon beside the snow globe should visibly break a condition; a new arrangement should visibly restore it. The appeal must come from readable reactions and satisfying puzzle construction.

**Strongest independent challenge:** “objects affect neighbors” is already central to inventory games, while “place everyone so their conditions are satisfied” is central to Is This Seat Taken?. A box, dragon, and snow globe can amount to a theme swap. Neither magical cargo nor warm/cold effects establishes a valuable mechanical gap. Do not promote distinctiveness until players can explain what this delivery puzzle offers beyond packing shapes or satisfying static preferences. The famous comparator also has an established publisher and a larger presentation/content package; its review response is not an audience we automatically reach.

**Four-to-six-day product estimate, conditional on strict cuts:** one Windows build, a single 2D crate scene, six parcel silhouettes using **three rule families**, **12 authored contracts**, undo/reset, effect preview, readable icons with text alternatives, a short introduction, local completion save, volume/display options, a final ending, coherent small original art/audio set, packaged QA, and draft screenshots/capsule/trailer materials. No 3D physics, variable crate shapes, procedural campaign, shop, collection economy, narrative chapters, voice acting, achievements, localization, or live challenges. Twelve contracts are a content cap, not a verified playtime promise.

Planning allocation is roughly one agent day for core interaction; one for rule clarity, undo/save and introduction; one to two for authoring and verifying contracts plus art/audio; and one to two for presentation, compatibility/package QA and store materials. This leaves little room for a failed interaction test. A four-day completion is the optimistic bound; a polished commercial campaign is not supported by this estimate. Store review/onboarding and any external launch waiting periods remain separate calendar time.

The biggest production risk is **puzzle authorship**. Reusing a board and code does not guarantee twelve distinct deductions, a good difficulty curve, or freedom from trivial alternative solutions. If six good contracts require adding new mechanics, the premise of cheap reusable content has failed. The other risk is preview solving the puzzle for the player: red/green feedback can make random rearrangement more efficient than reasoning.

**Estimated owner attention:** four to six hours through a finished microgame: roughly 1–1.5 hours recruitment/scheduling, 1–1.5 hours playtests, 0.5–1 hour art/audio and copy review, and 1–2 hours final acceptance and storefront handling. Recruitment availability is unknown. Reserve another 1–2 hours for launch-month support if released; this is a planning allowance, not observed support demand. No cash allocation or spend is authorized; fees and any paid assets remain separate unknown inputs. No revenue scenario is warranted from this evidence.

**One cheap falsifiable test, if later authorized:** create a paper/clickable crate using the six silhouettes and three rule families, then observe five target puzzle players over three short contracts. Continue only if at least four explain the effect of a move without coaching after the introduction, at least three voluntarily request another contract, and the designer can then author six additional nontrivial, solved contracts with the same rules in two hours. Record whether players reason or shuffle until all indicators turn green. Failure on authoring or clarity means simplify/hold before a production pass. Planning envelope: 0.5–1 agent day and about 1–1.5 owner hours for the test itself, with participant recruitment separately scheduled. These are discovery thresholds, not statistical validation.

**Audience/discovery hypothesis:** people seeking short, untimed logic sessions and satisfying organization. A three-puzzle demo and a clip showing a failed then successful cargo reaction could communicate the promise through Steam's puzzle/cozy audience and small puzzle-focused creators. No creator relationship, audience access, posting, or outreach is established. Test a **USD 3.99–4.99** price question only against the eventual content and polish; it is neither a chosen price nor a claim of willingness to pay.

## Tidepool Turn

**Positioning hypothesis:** a five-by-five miniature strategy board where the next tide visibly relocates the pieces. The potentially meaningful distinction is **planning a placement for its forecast destination**. Ecology is already a competitor promise; periodic water consequences already exist. The distinctive relocation still needs a playable check and a wider mechanic search before any uniqueness claim.

**Four-to-six-day estimate needs a narrower product:** three species plus fixed rocks, one deterministic horizontal movement rule, exact ghost-board forecast, six-tide sessions on **six curated starting boards**, local best scores, immediate restart, concise scoring explanations, original simple 2D art/audio, settings/save, packaged QA and draft store materials. Tiles move at most one square if the destination is clear; no pushing chains, conflicting currents, predation, births, resource economy, habitat simulation, infinite generator, unlock tree, or online daily mode. A few boards are a finite score toy, not demonstrated endless replay. The existing larger implication of a living ecosystem should be removed from any product promise if this cut is used.

**Strongest challenge:** with so few species and placements, the game may be solved by following the ghost preview and repeatedly using one scoring pattern. Adding ecological cascades to rescue depth would directly increase the clarity, balance, art, and QA burden. A quiet visual transformation can make an attractive clip without producing consequential decisions. The bounded product is technically plausible, but the promised short-session replay value is materially less certain than Parcel's finite completion structure.

Estimated agent allocation: one day board/movement/forecast, one day input/scoring/explanations/save, one to two days board tuning and art/audio, one to two days packaging QA and store assets. Owner attention is approximately four to six hours plus a separate 1–2-hour launch-month support allowance, with the same unknown recruitment/store calendar constraints as Parcel. Cash remains unallocated. **USD 3.99–4.99** is a content-dependent price hypothesis; the currently discounted Preserve offer is a relevant pressure on value perception.

**One cheap falsifiable test, if later authorized:** use a paper board with three species and one tide rule; show five target players two six-tide scenarios. Continue only if four can predict/explain the next destination, three choose a placement specifically because of the forecast, and three voluntarily choose a third run. Record whether a single repeated pattern dominates both scenarios. Hold or redesign if the forecast is only decorative, choices are trial-and-error, or the same pattern wins regardless of board. Estimated test: 0.5–1 agent day and 1–1.5 owner hours, recruitment separately scheduled.

Audience/discovery is a hypothesis: compact board-strategy players who enjoy calm presentation and local score improvement. A readable before/after tide clip and two-board demo could reach nature/puzzle discovery channels. The aesthetic should suggest a small tactical toy; promising a simulation would attract expectations this scope cannot meet.

The 0.15 gap is fragile. If Parcel cannot produce a worthwhile contract set inside the content cap, scope falls to 2 and its score becomes **2.35**, below Tidepool. If its relocation proves closely duplicated or visually confusing, distinctiveness 2 reduces it to **2.25**. Price acceptance, actual owner time, production quality, rights/title clearance, individual-review friction, and reach remain unmeasured for both. The next useful evidence is a small interaction/content test after the owner chooses a direction, not another popularity total.
