## Option 1: Glass Court — active wall-rally arcade

**Hypothesis, intended player:** people who enjoy brief action score attacks and mastering expressive ball control. Padel supplies the interesting interaction: a wall rebound is an opportunity to recover position, not automatically a failed shot. We borrow that interaction without promising a realistic padel simulator.

**Hook:** dash around a tiny glass court, intercept the ball or let it rebound behind you, and time the return to chain shots through glowing wall sections. A bank shot builds the multiplier, but the resulting angle becomes your next recovery problem. The ball stays live throughout; this is continuous movement and timing, not a turn-based angle puzzle. The recognizable moment is a ball sailing behind the player, rebounding off the glass, and being returned at the last instant.

**Compact loop:** serve → sustain a 60–120-second rally → recover from awkward ricochets → miss ends the chain → instantly retry or change challenge. Fixed, authored wall-target patterns and a controllable late/early swing make skill improvement legible. Local medals and personal bests provide replay; no metaprogression shop is needed. Playing it after padel interest fades should still mean learning a satisfying movement/return system.

**Working price hypothesis:** $3.99–4.99. Higher prices up to the user's $9.99 ceiling are not justified for our proposed content merely because a more substantial comparable charges them.

Owner time includes three short feel sessions plus final build and pitch review. No content library is assumed ready.

**Cheapest fail test (proposal, not performed):** after one day, three fresh players each get ten minutes without coached timing. Stop if two cannot tell whether a miss was caused by position or swing timing, or if two finish with no desire to try another run. A second stop: if the 10-second capture communicates only generic Breakout, the distinctive bank-and-recover interaction has not landed. Current opportunity confidence **low-medium**; trend growth is better evidenced than paid demand for this exact small format.

## Option 2: Kettle Ridge — carrying and balance challenge

**Hypothesis, intended player:** physical-comedy and movement-challenge players who want a compact solo game with forgiving retries. Social hiking supplies the premise: somebody has to bring the absurd picnic load up to the group's summit camp. The transferable appeal is awkward movement, recoverable near-disaster and a tangible arrival, not a screen replacement for hiking.

**Hook:** carry an oversized camp kettle up a narrow hillside, counterleaning to keep the load upright as steps, slopes and gusts threaten to tip it. Reaching the top with the tea still inside is the goal. It is a movement game with load-induced balance, not inventory packing. A last-second recovery that saves the remaining tea is the trailer moment.

**Compact loop:** attempt one 8–12-minute authored ridge with three rest ledges → adjust movement and counterlean through tricky passages → spill some tea or fall to the last ledge → reach the camp → retry for a full-kettle medal or faster arrival. The full-kettle route is harder than simply finishing. Difficulty presets can widen the balance tolerance without changing the course. Replay rests on mastery and recoveries rather than a large world, loot table or changing scenery.

**Working price hypothesis:** $3.99–4.99 for one deliberately compact course plus challenge variants. The loop must earn that price through controls, feedback and repeat attempts. A nice view and a short walk are insufficient.

This is the higher implementation risk of the two options. Owner input is intermittent but must include three fresh hands-on sessions, difficulty and frustration decisions, and final package review.

**Cheapest fail test (proposal, not performed):** three fresh players get ten minutes with the greybox. Stop if failures feel like arbitrary physics, spills are hard to read, or satisfying control demands full ragdoll/foot simulation. Also stop or reprice if a single completion exhausts the appeal: one short scenic course cannot support the promised purchase merely by adding medals. Current opportunity confidence **medium-low**, with better price-format sales evidence than Glass Court but a much weaker transfer from the co-op hit and higher production risk.

## Handoff

These are two contrasting examples to offer alongside other categories, not greenlit recommendations. Prefer **Kettle Ridge for the broader examples list** if the aim is to show a non-puzzle, physical-comedy direction; prefer **Glass Court for the cheapest controlled action experiment**, with its limited commercial evidence stated. Neither is a proven 4–7-day release, and no fun has been tested.
