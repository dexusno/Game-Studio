# Next game research: eight directions across genres

Research date: **2026-09-06**. Research only; no game selected or built.

**Recommendation: test Scrapstorm first, Whisk Rush second, and False Signal third.** These offer three different player experiences: active combat, hands-on service, and a deduction thriller. Glass Court is the cheaper pure-action alternative; A Good Mend is the most tightly bounded craft option. This is an experiment priority, not a sales prediction or a claim that fun is established.

The owner's price ceiling is **USD 9.99**, conditional on credible genre/price demand and a worthwhile product within a few days of work. The evidence supports paid markets across these genres. It does **not** establish that our smaller versions can charge USD 9.99 or match the successful examples in a week. All prices below are test hypotheses; implementation estimates need a successful day-one prototype.

## Eight options to choose from

These are working names. All eight are shortlisted for comparison, with low overall confidence and unverified current trend timing. Historical topic growth is separately documented below.

|---|---|---|---|---|

The final build estimates include the prototype, finite content, modest original art and audio, saves/settings, packaged Windows QA and draft store media. They assume one offline PC platform and immediate progress on the core interaction. The six-to-seven-day options sit at the upper end of “a few days”; overruns should trigger cuts or a hold, not an automatic larger project.

## What makes the few-day estimates defensible enough to test?

They are conditional scope estimates, not demonstrated throughput. All eight still need human play evidence.

- **Scrapstorm:** after independent challenge, cut to one arena, two ordinary enemy types plus a simple finale, uniform controlled debris behaviour and **6–8 upgrades**. Avoid general debris physics. Day one tests whether both shielding and throwing are useful. Stop if perpetual shielding or constant firing dominates; more content will not repair that.
- **Whisk Rush:** one counter, four recipes, three shifts and limited score replay. Fake readable fill graphics replace fluid simulation. Day one tests whether batch freshness changes preparation and service decisions, and whether whisking stays pleasant after twenty drinks.
- **False Signal:** **six substantial calls in one shift**, three fair clue rules, at most two question choices, one persistent consequence flag, fixed audio and equivalent captions. Cut runtime AI, microphone input, a walking environment and branching campaign. Day one needs three calls with one finished-direction audio exchange. Stop if players cannot explain the evidence or the questions become rote.
- **Glass Court:** one 2D court, one ball, six challenge patterns, arcade trajectories and personal bests. No opponent AI, realistic spin, multiplayer or sports licence. Day one must make misses understandable and bank-and-recover visible.
- **Kettle Ridge:** one short side-view ridge, three checkpoints, explicit balance model and simple tilt-based tea loss. No fluid simulation or ragdoll. Day one must produce deliberate recoveries rather than random-looking falls.
- **A Good Mend:** one 2D work surface, three cloth sets and at most 18 repair cards; no real cloth physics or garment editor. Test meaningful route choices and authoring time before multiplying puzzles.
- **Good Dog Shift:** one yard, three temperaments, few activity stations, play/rest and limited upgrades. No breed catalogue, adoption story, disease system or freeform construction. If the first three dogs are not expressive, broader management cannot carry it.
- **Override Dispatch:** independent challenge cut three layouts/six shifts to **one fixed graph, three curated shifts and two visible complication types**. Begin with deadlines; add charging only if it creates a distinct choice. Bots should normally work competently. Stop if players want to drive everything manually or describe fixing stupid AI.

Typical directional test: three to five available target players, a short uncoached session, explain a consequential choice, and observe voluntary retry. Exact stop criteria are in each opportunity JSON. Small samples guide design; they do not estimate conversion. Owner hours cover intermittent playtests, decisions and presentation/build review; external recruitment and observation add time if players are not readily available.

No money has been committed. Existing machine/tools are assumed, and ongoing compute/subscription costs are unmeasured. A potential Steam app fee and any optional asset commissioning must be considered separately from owner hours; no purchase has been made. Reserve roughly 1–2 additional owner hours for launch-month triage, with actual support demand unknown.

Calendar time is separate: [Steam's Coming Soon documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon), checked Sep 6, requires at least two weeks of public Coming Soon visibility. [Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect) also describes onboarding/review and initial fee waiting periods. A six-day build is not a six-day launch promise.

## How a trend could help this game sell

The proposed path is **recognisable inspiration → a clear gameplay clip or demo → interested players → a purchase decision**. Each step is a hypothesis for our game. A search-friendly title may help, but a good name, clear genre promise and an appealing ten-second action matter more than forcing a keyword into the title.

For Scrapstorm, show household junk becoming protection and then a risky attack. For Whisk Rush, show an understandable pour-and-rescue moment. For False Signal, show two contradictory callers and a fair choice. These concepts can be introduced to relevant game audiences as well as people following the broader topic. No creators have been contacted, no audience access is established, and no organic-conversion rate is assumed.

Before expanding production, the selected prototype should supply a readable capture and a bounded demo. Check whether target players understand the action, choose to continue and consider the proposed price appropriate. The finished experience must justify its price even if all trend traffic disappears.

## Independent challenge and retained decisions

The scout and analyst owned separate notes. The analyst challenged the root's Scrapstorm and Override Dispatch concepts, reducing both content envelopes and pointing out existing multi-vehicle dispatch competition. That changed the current candidate records. It supported Scrapstorm as the stronger experiment and identified incompetent automation as Override's likely failure mode. The earlier False Signal challenge reduced a twelve-case campaign to six calls and required fair captions/audio.

Rubric scores remain in the opportunity JSONs. They are weak-evidence comparison aids, not expected sales. The three recommended experiments reflect genre variety, a visible hook, paid-format support and what a one-day test can resolve; they are not mechanically sorted by a fraction of a point.

- **Postcard Press:** held as a paper-puzzle reserve, with older trend windows and strong masking/ordering overlap.
- **Scrap Swing:** held as an evergreen cargo-arcade alternative. It was initially derived from games; it is not silently renamed into trend evidence. Scrapstorm instead uses a new shield/attack loop prompted by miniature-from-waste interest.
- **Parcel Paradox:** held after close overlap with packing/adjacency games; no independent trend origin established.
- **Tidepool Turn:** held after overlap with tile-ecology/spatial games and unclear tide decision value; terrarium interest alone does not validate its mechanic.
- **Latchline:** held as an evergreen control experiment; no verified trend origin.

Next concrete action is to choose **one** of these research options for its day-one playable test. The recommendation is Scrapstorm, with the stop criteria above. No prototype has been started.
