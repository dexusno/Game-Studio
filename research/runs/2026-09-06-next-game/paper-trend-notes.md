# Paper hobby discovery: Postcard Press

**Access date: 2026-09-06.** This is a new direction derived from paper-hobby evidence, independently of Parcel Paradox. It proposes one experiment; no game selection, implementation, budget allocation, account action, or public release is authorized here. The producer owns candidate records and shared status.

**Recommendation:** retain **Postcard Press** as a plausible candidate born from the trend scan. Its hook is: **stamp one postcard; peel off the paper masks and use their printed scraps to finish the second.** The durable appeal would be a small visual deduction followed by a satisfying reveal. A name that matches search terms is unnecessary. The trend inspires the material, ritual, and audience hypothesis; it does not excuse an uninteresting game.

## The proposed interaction

**Postcard Press** is a working title, with no name clearance. The game presents two tiny unfinished postcard pictures. On the first card, position a reusable paper mask, press one or two shaped stamps, then peel. Ink on exposed card stays behind; ink caught on the paper mask stays on that scrap. Move and rotate the printed scrap onto the second card to complete both pictures. The puzzle is deciding where to hide ink now so that the useful leftover appears in the other picture. Undo and restart permit experimentation without a timer.

The first ten seconds should show one press producing two useful parts: a stamped landscape on the first card, then a peeled, printed scrap becoming the missing shape on the second. The cards are tiny original graphic compositions built from a few shapes and two inks, not letters requiring prose or a catalog of illustrated customers. The proposed pairing is an original studio design hypothesis, not a claim that stamping, masking, negative space, or collage is an unprecedented mechanic.

The evergreen reason to play is recognizing how one action solves two connected visual problems, then seeing the clean reveal. If no meaningful choice survives removal of the paper aesthetic, hold the concept. Freeform decoration, stories about correspondents, online penpals, postal delivery simulation, and a shop economy are outside this proposal.

## One-day test and production envelope

**One agent day for a prototype, if authorized:** two small grids/canvases; one ink; two fixed stamp shapes; one rectangular removable paper mask; 90-degree rotation and grid snapping; retained ink on the mask; three known-solvable target pairs; undo/reset; short press/peel motion and sound. No procedural generation, arbitrary cutting, freehand drawing, paper physics, or interface library beyond what is necessary. One incomplete final-art interaction should still be inspected at play scale.

**Falsifier:** show it to five people who enjoy small logic/craft games, including at least two with a real paper hobby. After one example, continue only if at least four predict which ink will remain on the card versus the mask, at least three deliberately prepare the scrap for the second target rather than solve by random shuffling, and at least three voluntarily request a fourth pair. Ask the hobby participants what they would miss from doing this with physical paper; if screen escape is the main need and none finds the digital deduction appealing, lower demand rather than citing the trend again. Also require the designer to create six additional solved target pairs using the same tools in two hours; otherwise the content estimate fails. This is a directional discovery test, not a statistically validated market experiment.

**Four–six agent days for a finished microgame is plausible only with these cuts:** a single Windows desktop scene; **10 paired postcard puzzles**; three stamp shapes; two mask shapes; at most two paper scraps; two inks; rotation/snap rather than arbitrary geometry; short introduction, undo/reset and hints; local completion save, settings, coherent original art/audio, ending/gallery of completed cards, package QA, and draft store assets. Include the prototype in this total. One day interaction, one day clarity/save/UI, one to two days authored pairs and sensory polish, and one to two days QA/package/store materials leave little room for a failed test. No verified playtime claim can be made from ten pairs.

The second target makes the mechanic more distinctive but increases authoring and explanation burden. If removing the second card is required for clarity, reassess against Factory Balls instead of carrying over the distinctiveness score. Do not add several new stamp sets to disguise shallow puzzles. No endless mode, customer letters, collectibles economy, user accounts, sharing backend, localization, real mail service, or recurring content promise.

**Estimated owner attention:** approximately **4–6 hours** through a finished microgame, including recruitment/scheduling 1–1.5 hours, short playtests 1–1.5 hours, art/audio and copy review 0.5–1 hour, final acceptance/store handling 1–2 hours. The first test itself needs roughly 1–1.5 hours plus recruitment. A separate 1–2-hour launch-month support allowance is a planning reserve, not an observed need. Store setup/review/waiting time is separate calendar time. Cash remains unallocated; no paid assets or store fee committed.

A **USD 2.99–3.99** premium price question is a hypothesis to test after observing content and polish, not an approved live price. A short sample/demo is a plausible route to explain the mechanic. No units, gross revenue, net proceeds, or profit forecast is made. The strongest value risk is competing against inexpensive puzzle games with far more authored content.

## Comparison with Parcel and recommendation to the producer

Parcel asks for a valid arrangement of whole objects under neighbor conditions. Postcard Press asks for an operation and placement plan that divides ink between two related outputs. The latter comes from the paper trend's act of making and keeping, not a paper label added to Parcel. Both still face authored-puzzle cost, visual clarity, and unknown purchase interest.

Suggested rubric proposal: **demand 2, distinctiveness 3, fun potential 2, scope fit 3, distribution 3 = 2.60/5**. Demand is indirect analog-hobby evidence plus adjacent games; distinctiveness is only the coupled card/scrap mechanism; fun is unplayed; scope depends on the content cap; distribution has a concrete press/peel reveal clip and craft/puzzle demo route, but no established audience access. Confidence stays low; current trend timing stays unverified.

This ties the narrowed Parcel proposal in [puzzle-notes.md](puzzle-notes.md), rather than automatically replacing it. Postcard Press has a more natural connection to the independently observed hobby signal and a specific transformation hook; Parcel has simpler state explanation and an easier finite-content structure. If the second card is confusing or equivalent puzzles already exist, distinctiveness falls to 2 and Postcard Press becomes **2.40**. If the test supplies direct evidence of meaningful, enjoyable play, fun could later rise to 3, producing **2.80** under the other assumptions. Scores aid the comparison and do not select a game or forecast sales.
