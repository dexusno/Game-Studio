## Best natural translation: visible mending

**Working hook: “A Good Mend” (title clearance unknown).** Turn a ragged tear into the best-looking part of a garment. Route one contrasting thread through selected anchor points so the stitches secure both sides of the tear without covering a keepsake emblem; then pull the thread and watch the cloth draw closed, leaving your chosen repair pattern visible.

This follows from the reported interest in visible repair. The proposed interaction is mending itself, not a generic game with a sewing skin. **Hypothesis:** planning a repair, seeing its tension resolve, and keeping a small gallery of improved garments can satisfy both short logic-puzzle players and people attracted to craft materials. It still works if the fashion topic stops growing: repairing something damaged into something personal is the enduring player promise.

**Compact loop:** inspect one damaged cloth area and two simple requirements; preview a continuous stitch route; sew and tighten; see which edges are secured; undo or accept the finished repair; unlock the next short commission or revisit one with a different pattern. Several legal solutions should be possible. Do not ask players merely to trace the only glowing line. No time pressure, shop economy or procedural story is required.

**Cheapest next test, if selected later:** 0.5–1 agent day for three tears on one cloth surface, a single thread rule, undo and a clear tightening reveal. In a 15-minute owner session, check whether the player predicts what a stitch secures, makes a meaningful route choice, and voluntarily repairs a second item. Then sample four additional target players when available: continue only if four of five understand the repair rule without explanation and three choose another repair. If everyone simply traces the obvious path or enjoys only the reveal, the current puzzle is too weak; change the mechanic before generating content. Fun has not been tested.

**Estimated commercial envelope: 4–6 focused agent working days**, contingent on that first test. One offline Windows build; mouse input; three reusable cloth/material sets; approximately 18 small authored repair cards; one stitch rule plus at most two modifiers; unlimited undo; a local completion gallery; legible thread contrast and optional patterns; restrained original cloth/needle audio; save, pause, focus, resolution and packaging checks. Allocate roughly one day interaction, one day content/rule checks, one day art/audio, one day UI/save/QA, and up to two days polish/defect contingency and truthful store media. Human review estimate: 1.5–2 hours across brief playtests and presentation review. Store lead time and any authorized fees remain separate. This estimate is unproven, not a launch commitment.

**Hard cuts:** no real cloth simulation, arbitrary sewing machine model, open-ended drawing tool, hundreds of garments, multiplayer, workshop, daily content obligation, lifelike stitch physics, controller/platform certification promise or ongoing story. Simulate the tightening outcome with a small predictable deformation/reveal, preserving the player's visible stitch route. If convincing material feedback or enough distinct puzzles cannot fit the envelope, hold it.

**Main production doubt:** a line puzzle may be easy to implement but fail to feel like sewing. Texture, thread rise, entry/exit cues, sound and the tightening reveal carry much of the value. Asset breadth cannot substitute for a meaningful repair decision. A low assumed price, perhaps USD 3.99–5.99 as a later experiment, does not establish willingness to pay.

## Alternative: miniature terrarium, held behind mending

The topic connection is natural, and it gives the shortlist a different subject from paper craft. **Possible hook, hypothesis:** build a tiny closed garden, then turn its lamp through one accelerated day and watch which arrangement shares water and light successfully. The glass container, condensation and miniature scale should be the central experience; this is not a renamed tidepool board.

However, a decoration-only version tends to require a large attractive asset library. [Terrarium](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3262910/Terrarium/), app **3262910**, released 2025-01-09, advertises over 100 decorations and a freeform bottle builder; its accessed header has only 15 reviews, 80% positive. This is a relevant low-response warning, not a sales estimate. Moving toward simulation adds ecological legibility and balance work, while fast deterministic rules may disappoint people attracted to real DIY gardens. A one-jar, three-plant, three-minute light/water test could settle this cheaply, but the loop is currently less specific than the mending choice.

Pressed flowers also deserves preservation as an inspiration lead, but this bounded pass did not find a compelling decision beyond arranging pretty pieces. “Miniatures from trash” could become clever object repurposing, yet a rich library of transformations would threaten the few-day budget. Neither is automatically rejected as a subject; both are held for a sharper interaction.

Recommendation to producer: carry visible mending as the best tested-by-research translation from this branch, with miniature terrarium as a brief alternative if portfolio variety warrants a separate interaction test. Keep overall confidence **low**, retain current-stage uncertainty, and use the trend as inspiration rather than as a claim that the resulting game is commercially validated. No game selection, prototype, spending, external contact or publication was performed or authorized by this note.
