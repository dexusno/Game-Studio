# Game release

Read [CHARTER](../../../studio/CHARTER.md), [WORKFLOW](../../../studio/WORKFLOW.md), and [TEAM](../../../studio/TEAM.md), then the game's `RELEASE.md`, `BUILD.md`, `QA.md`, manifest, and asset manifest. Establish the intended store, platforms, release artifact, and scope of existing authorization.

Use the `release-producer` to own store preparation and the `qa-playtester` to independently assess the exact release build. The `game-engineer` owns fixes and packaging support. Keep one writer for release records and one for each store asset; do not let concurrent workers submit or overwrite the same listing.

Verify current publishing terms from official store documentation. Record URLs and access dates for applicable fees, revenue share, onboarding, identity and payout information, content rules, ratings, technical requirements, and review timing. An individual account, sole proprietorship, public studio brand, and legal contracting identity are different concepts. Do not invent portal fields or infer that company registration is required from the word organization.

Prepare truthful store copy, supported languages and specifications, controls, screenshots or video from representative gameplay, pricing rationale, support details, and required credits. Treat target dates and commercial outcomes as plans or estimates. Generated concept art must not be presented as a gameplay screenshot.

Build from a known revision and record artifact location, version, platform, build command, and integrity hash where practical. Test install or unpack, first launch, core loop, input, audio, exit, and persistence when applicable on the actual release package. Record environment and evidence; distinguish failures, passes, and checks not run. Verify rights and attribution for included assets and middleware.

Finish all authorized local preparation before seeking missing input. Existing authorization persists: do not ask again for already authorized uploads or publishing. Where external publication, spending, credentials, contractual acceptance, or messages to others fall outside it, present the exact prepared action and the reason input is needed. Never substitute a checklist for a working release artifact or treat inaccessible store settings as completed.

For an authorized submission, use available tools, preserve receipts or submission identifiers, and verify the resulting store state. Record whether the build is prepared, uploaded, awaiting review, approved, scheduled, or live. A successful upload is not evidence that customers can buy or launch the game. Leave a specific next step and update the manifest only to the stage actually reached.
