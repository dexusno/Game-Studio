# Segmented shield — original mesh handoff

Authored 2026-09-08 with Blender 5.2.1 LTS by Codex for Klaus's Game Studio. The geometry is original procedural modeling from `../../scripts/create_segmented_shield.py`: designed curved armor cross-sections, overlapping ceramic plates, tapered cutting rims, short mechanical docks, bronze latches, countersunk fasteners and a gripped central mechanism. No downloaded mesh, purchased asset, stock kit, new AI image generation or external model service was used. Distribution follows the game's existing rights decision; this work adds no third-party geometry license or attribution obligation.

The three delivered FBXs are `SM_ShieldSegment.fbx`, `SM_ShieldHub.fbx` and `SM_ShieldSegmentGlow.fbx`. `asset-metadata.json` records exact material slots, dimensions, signed bounds, pivot, vertex-mask ranges and hashes. `export-checks.json` records checks of the actual exported FBXs after Blender reimport: signed axes/scale, origin, material ordering, one populated UV channel, outward winding of every disconnected closed part, and tangents. This evidence is a Blender asset check, not an Unreal import or gameplay test.

All three origins are the shield center `(0,0,0)`, in centimeters; face is `+X`, camera-facing mechanical back is `-X`. The base shard is centered on `+Z`, with a roughly 57-degree body and trimmed blade tips at 51 degrees. Its maximum radius is 37.9 cm. Duplicate the shard and matching inlay with rolls of `0,60,120,180,240,300` degrees about X. Segment and hub slots are `M_ShieldCeramic`, `M_ShieldBronze`, `M_DarkMetal` in that order; inlay slot 0 is `M_Core`. Vertex `Color` uses the established R=value, G=edge wear, B=recess/weathering, A=opaque convention. Runtime must move/hide/tint each inlay with its corresponding piece.

The hub remains held. Its Y-axis grip center is `(-12,0,-12)`, preserving the existing `SM_Forearm` placement. Its central socket accepts the existing `SM_Core` at `(-6,0,0)`. Replace the former `SM_ShieldPlate`; do not draw it behind these parts. The segment's shield-centered pivot is intentionally away from its visible centroid: its bounds center is approximately `(-0.794,0,24.818)`. Account for that offset if airborne movement/collision is centered on the visible shard rather than on the shield origin. No collision hulls are supplied; combat owns contact volumes.

The shared original palette and textures in `../generated/` are reused. The Blender scene packs the small existing texture dependencies and uses no external linked library. Existing original `SM_Core` and `SM_Forearm` appear only as fit references in the review scene; they are not new exports. The comparison image under the research run is a design reference, not geometry or a claimed gameplay result.

Four inspected Blender renders show the assembled back, assembled front, existing hand/grip fit and a single shard. They establish the new physical construction and attachment fit. The selected B art target still requires comparison in actual Unreal lighting, scale and motion; this source asset pass does not establish that the entire game's presentation meets B.

Reproduce from the project root with configured Blender: `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python games/dreambound/scripts/create_segmented_shield.py`. `-- --skip-renders` rebuilds meshes and runs the export checks without rendering; `-- --audit-existing` checks current FBXs and cleans/resaves the portable review scene. Only this directory is written. The integration owner imports into `/Game/Art/Meshes`, maps existing `/Game/Art/Materials`, updates the shared asset manifest and verifies actual runtime selection, launch, docking, depletion and regeneration.
