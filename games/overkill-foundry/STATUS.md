**Additive spread damage, selected 15 September:** When different eligible spreading parts reach the same extra robot, add their whole damage contributions. On a 20-damage bullet, contributions of 10 and 8 total 18 damage before defence. This replaces the draft rule that kept only the largest contribution. Preserve recipe-specific eligibility and explicit non-stacking restrictions; this does not copy every main-target effect to the extra target. Each eligible spreading part resolves its contribution as a separate hit on that extra robot: 10 and 8 are two hits, totalling 18 before defence. Evaluate per-hit defences and reactions for each qualifying hit using the state at that hit. A once-per-hit robot buff can therefore trigger twice if both hits qualify, subject to its own conditions and limits. Overlapping spread hits resolve in the order their contributing parts were placed in the bullet. Placing the 50% part before the 40% part on a 20-damage bullet gives 10 then 8; reversing their placement gives 8 then 10. Do not sort those hits by damage or recipe ID. If an earlier hit from the same bullet has killed a later spread hit's target, that later hit is lost. It deals no damage, triggers no hit-based reaction and is not automatically redirected. Fired parts remain consumed. Other explicitly triggered recipe effects retain their own rules. Apply the existing death-ends-fight rule: once player death is resolved, stop the remaining hit resolution and enter Game Over. Preserve already-resolved damage and the selected simultaneous-death outcome. This is a consequence of the settled rule, not a new owner decision to request. The arithmetic study now checks the accepted 10 + 8 = 18 example and preserves whole-number conversion per spread contribution.

**Percentage damage bonuses, selected 15 September:** Eligible percentage damage bonuses from different parts add together by default before the combined bonus is applied: +20% and +30% give +50%. Preserve explicit recipe conditions and non-stacking restrictions. This does not change bonus duration, effect targeting or the number of effect triggers. All damage is stored, previewed and resolved as whole numbers. The rework retains percentage bonuses and calculates their whole-number results using integer division, discarding remainders without carrying them into another hit or turn. Add eligible percentage bonuses before calculating the combined whole damage bonus once. For example, 9 damage with +60% gains 5 whole damage and resolves as 14. Split hits, damage reductions, recoil and conversions use the same whole-number rule. This is the chosen design rework under the owner's whole-number requirement, preserving existing bonus values; it does not replace percentages with flat bonuses. The [executable arithmetic study](design/analysis/whole_number_damage.py) passed 40,401 damage/percentage cases plus boundary examples. All 606 recipe rows and their printed costs/effect values are unchanged; no gameplay runtime exists.

**Player survival and simultaneous defeat, selected 15 September:** If the player and the last robot die in the same resolution, the outcome is Game Over. Direct HP costs or self-inflicted HP loss from effects the player uses must leave at least 1 HP. Damage returned by a robot's recoil buff is enemy damage, even when the player's attack triggers it, and can kill the player if it reduces HP to 0. The nonlethal rule for the player's own effects does not prevent robot recoil or other enemy damage. The seven existing player-HP-cost recipe drafts already state a minimum of 1 HP afterward. A recipe with player HP cost C requires at least C + 1 current HP to use. Pay the full HP cost; no partial payment is allowed. A 3-HP cost is unavailable at 3 HP or less; at 4 HP it leaves 1 HP. Printed recipe costs remain draft balance values.

**Part resale correction, 15 September:** each part has a base sale value derived from the normal resource cost of producing **one of that part through its main recipe**. Keep the selected current shop resource prices, 50% sale factor and whole-credit round-down: `floor(0.5 × sum(main-recipe ingredient quantity for one part × current shop resource unit price))`. Rare-recipe resource rebates or discounts do not reduce its sale value. Producing the same part through another recipe, in a batch or by copying also does not change its reference cost. Different outputs each use their own main-recipe value. **This supersedes the earlier actual-batch equal-cost allocation rule and resolves the pending mixed-output pricing question.** For example, a normal one-part ingredient value of 10 credits gives a 5-credit sale value even if an alternative recipe spends only 6 credits producing that part. Parts without a defined main recipe/one-part baseline need a content reference before pricing; do not invent it from the discounted producing recipe. Raw materials cannot be sold; energy-core and unused-crafted-part sales remain allowed. Selling removes the part while its recipe remains in memory. Utilities create no saleable Utility part, and unused parts/resources still clear at fight end without automatic sale.

## Settled design and remaining questions

**Substantive-gap audit, 15 September:** the core turn, survival, cooldown, storage and reward rules are substantially settled.
