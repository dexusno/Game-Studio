# Mercenary route fit — progression research note

**16 September 2026.** Bounded input to the campaign design, not a replacement progression specification or tested balance result. The owner requests different progression trees and three different cities for each mercenary, with semi-random robot attacks inside semi-steered progression. The names, topology shapes and placements below are proposals for that requirement. **Character-specific cities are our design, not an observed Slay the Spire 2 feature.**

Reviewed the selected starter sets and draft character mechanics in [RECIPE-CATALOGUE.md](../RECIPE-CATALOGUE.md), plus [ENEMY-ROSTER.md](../ENEMY-ROSTER.md) and [ROBOT-EFFECTS.md](../ROBOT-EFFECTS.md). No recipe, source balance value or shared status record was edited. Library IDs beginning C1/C2/C3 indicate the existing draft difficulty bands here; they need not permanently identify one physical city once twelve cities are introduced.

## Source-backed principles and limits

Accessed **16 September 2026 through indexed wiki extracts**; direct opens of this wiki previously returned 403. Extracts may lag the live game, and no current executable was tested.

- [StS2 Acts](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Acts) describes act-specific enemies/events/bosses, an Ancient offering at the beginning, a boss at the end, and alternate first acts. **Inference for our design:** geographical identity and an act's mechanical emphasis can change while its broad progression role stays legible.
- [StS2 Monsters](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Monsters) describes an easier pool for the first three ordinary fights in Act 1 and the first two in later acts, followed by a harder pool. **Inference:** introduce a city's new threat in controlled formations before combining it with other pressures. Those counts refer to monster fights, not necessarily the first three map nodes.
- [StS2 Map Locations](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Map_Locations) describes healing at Rest Sites and Ancient visits, different rewards for Elites, and a Rest Site before each boss. **Critical difference:** our owner selected HP carryover and no automatic transition healing or direct shop healing. Do not copy the source's combat density while implicitly counting on those recovery systems. A lower-risk route conserves HP; it does not secretly restore it.

Use the source's *graduated pressure and visible path tradeoffs*. Do not import its deck draw, card energy, healing schedule, floor count or character route ownership. Twelve encounters per city is a reasonable proposed fixture within the selected 10–20 range, not a source-derived optimum.

## What each starter can actually do

All mercenaries have SH001–SH008. Immediate part-supplied damage includes SH001 (6), SH003 (3), and SH004 (4, or 8 against an attack intent). SH002 supplies 6 prepared Shield; SH006 supplies 4 and protection from the first Burn/Thermal Runaway tick. SH005 provides spreading with cooldown 1. SH007 removes cooldown counters; SH008 proposes delayed extra gathering. The two cooldown recipes at entry are SH005 and SH008: cooling never refreshes the once-per-turn use of a no-cooldown starter. Extra gathering expires if the fight ends before it arrives.

## Four distinct branching structures and twelve city identities

Each structure is a **seeded family of stage graphs**, not one fixed path. Rejoining a stage does not collapse ordinary encounter selection to one option: every ordinary choice still presents three offers, at least one Regular. The final boss replaces those offers under the existing rule. Mayor meetings and shop revisits must not silently inflate the encounter count.

| Mercenary | Three unique city proposals | Branch shape and player tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Mara** | **Cinderwall** — furnace districts; **Coilbridge** — industrial transport bridges; **Glassward** — civic surveillance towers. | **Long fork:** three lanes persist for a short stretch, then reconverge before the next district. Choose predictable heavier single targets, lighter formations, or an optional Officer/Mystery emphasis; preview enough of the next stretch to make commitment meaningful. Vary formations and lane order per seed. |
| **Ivo** | **Brinegate** — salt-damaged harbor machinery; **Sablecross** — culverts and night freight channels; **Veilcourt** — concealed sensor estates and signal vaults. | **Braided routes:** frequent cross-links permit switching between exposed patrols, protected formations and preparation-friendly encounters. Branches change what is likely ahead rather than guaranteeing Acid Etch superiority. Mixing lane options supports both patient and direct-damage Ivo builds. |
| **Ada** | **Rivetford** — residential repair districts; **Latchhaven** — modular transit and salvage depots; **Relaykeep** — emergency-service command infrastructure. | **District circuits:** choose a short circuit, return to a shared junction, then choose the next circuit. Loops are visual routes through new encounters, never repeatable reward farming. Circuits differ in swarm/control work, single-chassis defence and optional Officers; no free Bolt repairs at a junction. |
| **Noor** | **Copperwake** — tidal power works; **Stormrail** — exposed electrified viaducts; **Prismhold** — light-routing towers and sealed substations. | **Expanding diamonds:** alternate short wide forks and longer narrower branches, reconverging at district boundaries. Choose more predictable preparation opportunities or sharper immediate pressure. The shape changes by seed within the same stage budgets; it grants no free Charge between encounters. |

At a convergence, lower-risk Regular options must remain reachable at low HP. Do not let an earlier branch commit the player to an undisclosed mandatory Officer sequence. A Mystery is not automatically safer: show known potential HP costs and allow its defined refusal option. Exact map edges, encounter count and probability implementation belong to the main progression document.

## Fairness constraints for semi-random moves and formations

1. **Randomise inside a role.** Sample a compatible formation and legal next move from a seed, with each stage retaining its learning goal and difficulty band. Do not randomly add Armor, filters or recoil to a supposedly familiar unprotected target without preview.
2. **Commit the shown intent.** Choose before player collection, then keep it stable while planning. Damage changes from player effects update the preview; crafting must not secretly reroll the attack. Continue uses the selected fight-entry state and seed; the same choices reproduce the same sequence.
3. **Preserve obligations and openings.** Charge must still precede its advertised blast; Recovery must occur when earned; finite summons stay finite; a no-attack preparation move cannot randomly turn into a surprise attack. Semi-random selection may change a legal branch, not erase the counterplay that made that branch fair.
4. **Bound unlucky streaks.** Do not allow every robot in a squad to independently roll its heaviest move on entry or repeatedly stack its suppression. Constrain combined encounter pressure and repeat windows. Exactly which limits are needed requires costed fights; independent uniform random selection is not evidence of uniform progression.
5. **Vary favourable and demanding encounters.** Among each ordinary three-offer choice, keep at least one Regular and avoid making every available formation attack the same build feature. Across a city, include roles rewarding big bullets, small hits/target splitting, and defence. Do not secretly inspect the player's build to generate counters.
6. **Use cumulative HP evidence.** At low HP, a reduced-risk branch should reduce expected damage exposure without guaranteeing survival or granting undeclared healing. Do not make Reactive Mesh an unavoidable final gate for a player whose remaining HP cannot pay its minimum successful direct hits. Healing recipes or max-HP upgrades may exist, but a random offer is not a guaranteed repair budget.

## Material prerequisites before balancing these routes

Reconcile MA004 and other immediate-Utility Shield dependencies, preserve the ban on independent Mara/Noor trait damage, and apply FX06 retaliation to Ada for qualifying Bolt support hits rather than silently assigning it to Bolt. Keep Ivo's saved-part checks within fights. First test common starter-only defence and damage, then the character additions, before assuming any rare recipe or Mayor choice.

Record minimum and typical HP loss across a complete sample city, including an unlucky legal move sequence, with no automatic healing. Compare an accessible Regular route with an optional Officer-heavy route under the same seed and initial HP. Track affordability and cooldown legality, not just average damage. These checks will distinguish useful variety from a campaign that becomes a string of counters to its own mercenary.
