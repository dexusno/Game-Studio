# Slay the Spire 2 enemy inspiration — Acts 1 and 2

Research date: 16 September 2026. Scope: both Act 1 variants, Overgrowth and Underdocks, plus Act 2, Hive. This is a bounded source study for Overkill Foundry, not an approved roster or balance specification. Robot names and adaptation rules below are proposals. The approved Scrap Drones + Siege Robot encounter remains unchanged.

## Evidence and access

Direct web opens of the three act pages returned HTTP 403. Search indexing returned substantial extracted article text, including enemy descriptions, move tables, notes and update histories. All observations below are **verified against those indexed extracts**, not a successfully opened live wiki page or a running game. Small search-result headlines alone were not sufficient evidence for a mechanic. Access date applies to every source in this file.

Confidence is medium for the published mechanical patterns and lower for their current release-branch applicability or exact numerical tuning. Some extracts were crawled months earlier. In particular, the Soul Fysh page has a beta-content warning and references August 2026 changes. Hive still lists Tunneler in encounter tables while its version history says the normal encounter was removed in v0.103.0; do not adopt that table as an exact current encounter pool. Recent enemy pages and their current pattern sections take precedence over older patch-history descriptions. No Slay the Spire 1 enemy mechanics were silently substituted.

The [Acts index](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2%3AActs) identifies Overgrowth and Underdocks as alternate first acts and Hive as the second act. The [Overgrowth](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2%3AOvergrowth), [Underdocks](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2%3AUnderdocks) and [Hive](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2%3AHive) pages establish the encounter categories used below. The indexes were used for coverage and categorization; the individual articles supply the mechanics.

## What transfers well

These are design inferences from the observed patterns:

- Teach a small set of pressures singly, then combine them: escalating damage, priority targets, a defensive window, and choosing many small hits versus a large hit.
- Offset enemy cycles so the player chooses which next attack to prevent. A mixed group need not have every robot attack simultaneously.
- Make a buff change the best allocation of parts, not simply add more HP. Finite damage gates reward small hits; Armor rewards combining parts; recoil discourages repeated hits while active.
- Escalation makes stockpiling costly. Our unlimited material/part storage and fully visible recipe memory mean a setup window is substantially stronger than an equivalent card-draw window.
- Translate deck pollution into clearly shown temporary combat interference only when it creates a useful choice. Do not add a deck, energy, a general action limit or an automatic turn-ending trigger.
- A copied numerical curve would not be balance evidence: our multiple shots, uncapped part assembly, Shield timing and global cooling change both offence and defence.

## Suggested shortlist for integration

These are recommendations, not owner decisions or a new implementation scope.

For City 1, the most portable patterns are predictable Patrol Mule cycles, the existing Armor Siege Robot, a Firewall Coil Sentinel, the staggered Redline Pursuer, and a finite-protection boss inspired by Vantom. Add one priority-target formation or a clearly announced second wave to test target allocation. The original drone/siege walkthrough remains the basic reference encounter.

For City 2, begin with Cargo Crew formations, Feedback Pylon windows, Brood Press spawning and Coupled Hauler synchronized defeats. Twin Gantry provides a boss that combines target allocation, burst timing and defence without requiring a deck. More intrusive candidates — recipe surcharges, per-use penalties, theft, damage caps and fatal clocks — deserve separate paper examples before becoming baseline content.

The useful opposing tests are **Armor versus Fuse Cells**, **Runaway Motor versus finite setup windows**, **Feedback Pylon versus rapid separate shots**, and **Brood Press / Coupled Hauler versus single-target burst**. These pressures can reveal whether one recipe strategy solves every encounter, while retaining the owner's freely composed bullet and End Turn control.

## Boundaries and unresolved conversion work

- This research covers tactical examples across all nine Act 1/2 bosses and all nine elites, plus selected ordinary enemies. It is not an exhaustive ordinary-enemy transcription.
- Buff/effect names in the adaptation column are provisional original working names. Shared names and exact semantics should be reconciled by the integration owner with the existing recipe catalogue, character systems and the other research agent.
- **Armor remains our established per-hit flat reduction.** StS2 Plating grants Block over turns; it is not mechanically equivalent despite the earlier naming discussion.
- Ward consumption, target-locked interference, temporary disable states, spawning and damage-gate rules need explicit tooltips in any later specification. They are content conversions, not permission to reopen settled whole-number, Shield, cooldown, death or Escape rules.
- No runtime was changed, no combat balance was validated, and no playtest was performed. No source artwork or prose was copied into the game content.
