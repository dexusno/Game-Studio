# Slay the Spire 2 progression study

Research date: 16 September 2026. Purpose: inform enemy placement and controlled variation in Overkill Foundry's **four mercenary campaigns, each with its own three cities — twelve city identities in total**. That campaign structure is Klaus's requirement, not a feature attributed to Slay the Spire 2. This note supplies research and recommendations; the integrated campaign document owns actual city names, placements and selected rules.

## Access and confidence

The requested starting point is the [Slay the Spire 2 main wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2%3AMain). Direct page requests were blocked or returned fetch errors; earlier requests in this research session returned HTTP 403. Meaningful search-index extracts supplied article sections, tables and patch notes. This is indexed-text verification, not live game testing. All sources below were accessed on 16 September 2026. Some crawls were weeks or months old, so exact current-branch placement remains less certain than the broad progression structure.

Searches repeatedly returned **Slay the Spire 1** pages titled Map Generation, Map Locations, Monsters and Events. Their 17-floor templates, map percentages and repeat rules are **not** STS2 evidence. No usable STS2-specific Map Generation article or full current generation algorithm was retrieved. STS2 namespace articles and STS2 patch notes are identified explicitly below. Warnings or inconsistencies in those pages remain material limits.

## Practical progression recommendations

The following are recommendations for the integrated route proposal. They preserve the owner's ordinary three-offer choice, at least one Regular option, and approximately 25% Officer/Mystery appearance preferences. They are not a recommendation to replace that system with an STS map.

### Common pacing envelope, different campaigns

Use a shared progression vocabulary across all twelve cities: introduction, development, pressure and final boss. Keep independent city identity, formations, Officer assignments, boss choices and Mayor offers for every mercenary route. Sharing the pacing vocabulary or underlying robot family does not make the locations interchangeable.

Each character's City 1 must teach its own route rather than assume the player completed the previous character's entire campaign. The established character unlock is a city victory with the preceding character. Later mercenary routes should offer different problems, not simply inflate HP because they unlock later. Character-specific mechanics remain draft designs; no route should require an unselected mechanic or a rare cleansing recipe to be viable.

For City 1, use three selected introductory Regular fights as the reference learning window. For Cities 2 and 3, use two introductory Regular fights to show the new city and the new Mayor upgrade. This borrows the source's idea, not its mandatory node sequence. Count those completed battles independently of selected noncombat Mystery encounters. If Officers become eligible only after that window, record it as a new pacing proposal and apply the appearance preference to eligible offers instead of pretending the probability never changed.

The development band introduces one new pressure at a time, then pairs it with a familiar robot. The pressure band combines two already understood pressures, offers more demanding Officers and previews the city's boss problem. Do not require every city to add an entirely new status system. Distinct formations, opening offsets, support dependencies and scenery can provide meaningful differences while preserving readable rules.

Use the selected city length to allocate these bands rather than copying STS floors. An encounter counter advances on a completed fight or counted Mystery encounter, not on an offer presentation, a shop reopening or a Mayor dialogue. The final boss remains the last counted encounter. Repeatedly choosing the Regular alternative must remain legal through every city length supported by the design.

### Offer selection and encounter variety

Separate **what can be offered** from **what the player actually chooses**. An Officer appearing in roughly a quarter of offer sets does not mean a quarter of the chosen encounters are Officers. Do not secretly force a quota by removing the Regular alternative. Likewise, a Mystery option's offer rate says nothing by itself about the rate of healing, combat or upgrades inside it.

For a first implementation proposal, use authored city-and-band formation pools, then seed their order and eligible selections. Each formation specifies its bodies, positions and opening intents. Avoid consecutive identical formations. Prefer unused formations and Officer identities, but define an explicit fallback if a long city exhausts its pool; do not assert the source's absolute no-repeat rule without enough content. Distinguish an enemy appearing again in a different squad from repeating the exact encounter.

Use forward-looking UI: show the selected boss identity or key threat early if that becomes the route specification, show each offered formation's meaningful threat, and keep shops/reward review available before the boss. This creates a preparation beat using existing systems rather than an unselected free heal or extra encounter. A safe alternative can be less demanding than an Officer without being a guaranteed zero-damage recovery fight.

### Semi-random attacks with stable difficulty

Klaus's requested variation fits three source-supported patterns: fixed openers followed by weighted legal moves; cycles started at different offsets; and state-dependent branching. Combine those within an authored move grammar for each robot. For example, a charging weapon may choose which lighter move precedes Charge, but its heavy blast always follows its displayed Charge; a summoner may choose an available helper, but obeys its lifetime supply and live-helper limit.

Randomize only eligible decisions at the prescribed selection point, then reveal and lock the intent before the player's planning. Do not reroll because the player crafts, changes targets, shops, reloads the interface or discovers a better shot. Reconstruct the same encounter-entry state and seed on Continue as already selected. Identical subsequent choices reproduce the same outcomes; altered choices may lead to different legal branches. Store or deterministically reconstruct chosen patterns so restarting is not a reroll opportunity.

Protect the difficulty envelope with visible restrictions: preserve charge-before-heavy, cap consecutive heavy actions, avoid synchronized heavy openers unless explicitly authored, keep recovery opportunities within a bounded cycle, and prevent a summoner from evading its finite supply. A city pressure budget can bound combined damage, Armor, recoil and support interference while allowing several formations. These are design controls, not a claim that the current roster's numerical budget has already been calibrated.

Keep generated moves truthful in the complete enemy-phase forecast, including earlier ally buffs. A new game may rearrange legal patterns; it should not change the meaning of Armor, cooldowns, Shield activation or enemy death. Stable progression means a comparable preparation burden and survivable counterplay for the expected city rewards, not identical HP loss in every seed.

### Attrition and reward checks

Our material/part carry rules apply inside fights, while HP carries across them and automatic healing is absent. Assess a whole city, not just separate fights started at full HP. Before accepting a placement curve, compare different steering choices, actual recipe memory, permanent upgrades, optional healing access and decisions to avoid Officers. Do not balance a route around always finding a particular rare upgrade or healing recipe.

Check both a player who takes nearly every Regular alternative and one who accepts several Officers. The former still needs enough opportunities to reach the boss; the latter needs meaningful reward upside without becoming the only viable route. Test short and long city lengths, rare-event streaks, repeated low-resource hauls, multiple-shot builds and deliberate saved-part play. No new fixed resource amount, healing service or recipe cost is selected by this recommendation.

## Specific remaining evidence limits

- Exact current STS2 map room percentages, graph widths, connection algorithm, minimum shop count and all elite/rest adjacency constraints were not established. No STS1 numbers should fill those gaps.
- Dated patch notes establish intended changes, not a guarantee that no later build altered them. Indexed descriptions are suitable inspiration, not executable map specifications.
- The source's first easy-fight counts and anti-repeat statements are observed rules. Our eligibility windows, encounter pools, weighted moves and fallback rules remain explicit proposals until integrated or selected.
- No gameplay runtime, recipe values, saved campaign state or existing shared handoff was edited for this research. No difficulty simulation, playtest or validated win-rate curve was produced.
