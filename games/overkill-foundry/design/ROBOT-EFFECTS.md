# Robot effects — roster draft

16 September 2026. Companion to the [enemy roster](ENEMY-ROSTER.md). Armor is the owner's selected name and mechanic. Other names and definitions below are proposed adaptations for review. All amounts are whole numbers. Nothing here changes the 606 recipe rows or supplies innate player gun damage.

## How to read the effects

An amount is not a duration: Armor 3 means three damage removed per hit, not three turns. Each timed application also states its expiry. A source-linked effect names the robot supplying it and ends when that source dies. Ordinary stored parts and recipes are never silently deleted by these effects.

For this roster draft, an enemy-applied effect lasting through the next player turn survives the current enemy-turn end, affects the following preparation/End Turn, and then expires. The exception is an amount consumed by a particular event, which clears when consumed. Permanent-in-fight counters end at fight end. Same-effect amounts add unless their row says otherwise; named auras from identical sources use the strongest surviving value, not a multiplied value. All of this is proposed content specification, not a claim that the owner previously chose a universal status system.

## Existing recipe vocabulary

These four new names are aliases for the existing catalogue definitions. They are not extra simultaneous copies of the same status. A coordinated recipe-text rename can follow review; until then, old recipe wording resolves to the same effect. Recipe-specific targets, conditions and transfer rules still apply.

| Existing draft term | Proposed name | Behaviour retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burn | Thermal Runaway N | On an enemy, after its action sequence, deal N damage through Shield then HP and reduce N by one. Tick once per round. On the player, retain the catalogue's after-player-action-phase tick. For this roster's timing proposal, that tick follows End Turn's Shield activation and precedes enemy actions. |
| Corrosion | Acid Etch N | On an enemy, remove N HP before its action sequence, then reduce N by one. On the player, tick at preparation start after old Shield resets and before fresh start-of-turn grants. This bypasses ordinary protection. Enemy-inflicted loss can kill; player-owned direct self-loss remains nonlethal under the settled rule. |
| Mark | Target Paint N | Adds N to the next main shot against a marked enemy, then clears. A mark applied by that shot's after-hit payload helps a later shot. On the player, add N to the next enemy attack's total, placing the addition in the first hit, then clear. This remains distinct from a per-hit damage multiplier. |
| Weaken | Drive Fault N | Subtract N from the next enemy attack's total, consuming the reduction across its hits in order, then clear. On the player, reduce the next main shot's calculated damage before target bonuses, then clear. Clamp every hit at zero. |

Existing counters are available: SH033 removes Burn/Corrosion, SH073 removes Weaken/Mark, SH022 and SH063 supply protection with relevant cleansing. Their draft timing/dependency corrections still apply; this roster does not invent a universal dispel button or require a particular cleansing recipe to win.

## Resolution conventions for this draft

Enemy actions resolve in their displayed left-to-right order. Select the move before the player's collection; ordinary damage/status changes update its preview, but crafting or firing never secretly rerolls the move. Show the complete enemy-phase forecast, including known preceding support buffs. Random intent selection is deferred until a later balance pass.

For direct damage, first assemble the part-supplied whole damage and existing recipe bonuses. Apply offensive flat suppression, then recipe-defined target bonuses, then Armor, then any Ablative Tile, then Shield and HP. A qualifying Reactive Mesh reaction follows that hit. Existing part-placement order controls spread contributions, and existing killed-target/lost-hit behaviour remains unchanged. New status payloads use the printed recipe order. This is a proposed consistent resolution order for the roster, not a replacement for an explicitly different recipe trigger.

Player End Turn unloads unused bullets and activates prepared Shield using applicable penalties, then resolves the player's Thermal Runaway tick. For each robot in action order, resolve its Acid Etch, then its action if still alive and eligible, then its Thermal Runaway if still alive; proceed to the next robot. A source death immediately updates later actors' linked effects. Finally apply the selected Shield reset/cooldown tick. Acid Etch can kill an enemy before its action; robots killed earlier do not act. Automatic cooldown ticks still skip the use round; none of these effects changes the owner's cooldown duration rule.

Direct enemy-granted protection in this roster uses Armor, finite tiles or source-linked effects. Enemy Shield grants are left out until their useful creation timing is specified; a Shield grant that immediately vanishes at enemy-turn end would not fulfil the protector role. Permanent player upgrades can still grant fresh Shield or explicitly retain it at their defined triggers.

## Source and migration notes

The two dated studies distinguish wiki observations from these adaptations: [Acts 1–2](research/2026-09-16-sts2-acts-1-2.md) and [Act 3/effects](research/2026-09-16-sts2-act-3-effects-mayors.md). Source effect families include [Buffs](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Buffs), [Debuffs](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Debuffs), [Afflictions](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Afflictions), and the individual enemies linked from the roster. Access was through indexed wiki extracts on 16 September 2026; direct pages returned 403.

These adaptations deliberately omit deck pollution, forced one-action turns, permanent recipe loss, maximum-HP destruction, per-turn boss damage caps and unavoidable instant-death clocks. That keeps the selected crafting rules intact and lets earned powerful builds end fights quickly. Mechanical counterplay is supplied by ordinary crafting, target order, stored parts and defence; optional cleansing improves it.

Before implementation, migrate the four effect aliases consistently across tooltips, recipe text and saves; reconcile the already-flagged Utility/Shield dependencies; and test the new resolution order with concrete hits. No save or runtime exists yet. A new name is not evidence that a mechanic is balanced.
