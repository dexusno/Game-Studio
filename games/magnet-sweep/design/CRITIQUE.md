# Magnet Sweep — independent gameplay critique

2026-09-11. This replaces the earlier review with the owner's requested focus: gameplay, enjoyment, rewards and replay. It challenges the previous timed-score design and records a revised recommendation after direct dialogue with the interaction and progression designers. No game has been played. These are design judgments and predictions, not demonstrated fun.

**Verdict: the previous concept was too weak to endorse as the game's identity.** It promised satisfying collection, then built its decisions around avoiding collection, emptying a small basket and losing upgrades. Three designers had converged on a coherent ruleset without establishing a compelling experience. The stronger direction is a tactile salvage toy with expressive pulls, retained magnet improvements and new arrangements that invite different satisfying actions.

## One coherent recommendation

Sweep loose scrap into a chunky magnet. Release to stop attracting while retaining the haul. Deliberately dump into a forgiving furnace: metal tumbles, the sound resolves, and its visible fill line advances. Any scrap contributes; enough remains on the tray that nobody must hunt the final fleck.

A rich tangle invites something more expressive. Latch its obvious ring, move away to stretch a tether, then release: the clump tears through the indicated corridor, collecting loose scrap and freeing another linked tangle. A straightforward pull works; a clever angle produces a spectacular chain. Reaching the furnace target completes the job after the current action finishes. Early completions visibly rebuild the magnet and grant an immediately usable improvement.

Continue with the earned rig on a differently arranged delivery. The promise is **collect satisfying loads, learn to pull better, and discover another impressive haul to make**. Default countdowns, compulsory resets and a separate extraction-puzzle objective do not belong in this recommendation.

## 1. Critical — The old magnet punished its own fantasy

**Design judgment:** Requiring active attraction to retain cargo makes scrap a hazard. With bits worth one point per space and cores worth two, a happy sweep can consume capacity needed for the obviously better material. Wide then enlarges this danger area. Tiptoeing around metal is a questionable foundation for a game selling the pleasure of collecting it. Higher value density does not by itself create interesting strategy; core-first may simply solve it once.

**Change:** Release retains cargo. Make all scrap useful by collected amount, without the two-to-one density trap. Heavy tangles differ through the action they invite and the haul they release. Wider attraction should feel like becoming more powerful.

**Test:** Does the player sweep confidently, or keep avoiding undesirable pickups? Does a heavy assembly suggest a fun pull rather than an obligatory superior item? If removing the penalty exposes a mindless loop, improve the action rather than reinstate frustration as depth.

## 2. High — A collection target must give purpose, not impose chores

**Design judgment:** I initially preferred recovering one named goal part. The designers correctly challenged it: players could rush that pickup, while blocking it behind linked supports could shift the game toward mechanical puzzles. A furnace fill line better connects every sweep to the same goal. A quantity target is not inherently bad; an arbitrary delay before the next reward is.

**Change:** Bank any scrap toward a visible forging target below the available total. Give ordinary hauls substantial, legible progress. Do not raise requirements merely to cancel earned magnet strength. There is no money/shop layer, mandatory full clearance or need to farm earlier jobs for essential improvements. Crossing the target must not interrupt a pull, cascade or satisfying deposit; let the player finish and choose to continue.

**Test:** Does dumping feel like accomplishing something tangible? Does the player finish with appealing opportunities left, or resent being sent back for a negligible remainder?

## 3. High — The tug could become waiting or precision busywork

**Design judgment:** Holding over every heavy object until a meter fills would replace collecting with waiting. The interaction designer's latch, move and release gives the player authorship over a direction. However, narrow corridors or mandatory exact chains could replace sweeping pleasure with fussy aiming.

**Change:** Use conspicuous rings, a readable path and forgiving width. Reward a direct pull; reward thoughtful positioning with a richer chain. A novice tears a clump free and feels strong. An interested player moves to the far side of a seam before releasing, drawing several pockets together. Let the player experience a cascade early rather than promising the interesting action behind prolonged collecting.

Remove the hard cargo limit and load slowdown. Forced unloading no longer supports the interesting choice, which is how to aim a satisfying pull. Show the carried contribution toward furnace completion so players can choose when to dump. Preview the entire affected linked group; avoid unannounced propagation that makes every pull clear the board.

**Test:** Can players anticipate and enjoy direct and ambitious pulls? Do sweeping and deliberate dumping remain satisfying between them?

## 4. High — One button can conceal several confusing actions

**Design judgment:** Returning an entire haul to its source on an off-pad release visibly undoes progress for a minor input error. Keeping cargo solves that problem, but contextual sweeping, latching and dumping can create new ambiguity.

**Recommended input contract:** Primary down over the highlighted furnace while carrying dumps. Otherwise, primary down on a highlighted ring latches that tangle. Otherwise, holding primary sweeps. A sweep does not unexpectedly become a latch when it passes a ring. While latched, mouse movement stretches the tether; release yanks. Releasing an ordinary sweep simply retains cargo. Present the pending action before the press, with generous furnace acceptance.

I initially preferred automatic banking. The interaction designer persuaded me that deliberately initiating the heavy dump preserves an important payoff, provided it is effortless to understand.

**Test:** Can a new player predict the next press/release in each context without instruction? Repeated surprise actions outweigh any theoretical elegance of using one button.

## 5. High — Upgrades must earn anticipation and attachment

**Design judgment:** Choosing radius or capacity twice creates configurations, not necessarily exciting builds. Basket can feel like buying relief; Wide can buy frustration. Resetting both after every short shift removes attachment before the player enjoys being powerful. A compulsory countdown similarly substitutes urgency for an established desire to continue.

**Change:** Keep improvements through the journey. The magnet itself is the visible restoration project: its new shape corresponds to a new collection capability. Ordinary radius or reach growth need not masquerade as a difficult branch choice. A behavioral milestone, such as freeing linked tangles in a cascade, should immediately change the next enjoyable action. Do not instantly neutralize it with proportionally tougher targets.

**Test:** Can players say what they want to do with the improvement, then do it? Do they willingly start the next delivery? A larger number or a compulsory next-job click does not establish rewarding progression.

## 6. High — Replay needs fresh opportunities after the rig is complete

**Design judgment:** Revisiting the first tray with a stronger magnet could be pleasant once and trivial thereafter. A finite upgrade journey alone does not meet the requested replayability. Neither does cosmetic random jitter. The fully improved tool must still have interesting things to do.

**Change:** Offer new deliveries within the same loop, remixing a bounded set of tangle positions, links, loose-scrap seams and approach spaces. Keep a comparable salvage supply and reachable fill target. Variation must change which direction offers a rich pull, which tangle opens another opportunity, or whether a broad sweep is appealing. Preserve straightforward alternatives; there should be several satisfying approaches, not one correct solution. Retain the rig and allow an exact-arrangement retry as well as another delivery.

The repeat invitation is “That seam could come loose in one enormous pull.” Start with positive per-pull feedback, without standing bests: comparisons can make ordinary sweeping feel like spoiling a record. Add comparisons only if actual play shows they help. Total delivery value saturates at full clearance; keep it descriptive.

**Test:** With upgrades already complete, show an unfamiliar arrangement before suggesting a tactic. Does the player spot a possibility, choose to try it and voluntarily request another delivery? If they merely repeat the same obvious motion at relocated targets, the remix has failed to create replay. This is a necessary unresolved risk, not grounds to declare the simpler campaign sufficient.

## Dialogue and evidence still required

Progression persuaded me to make the magnet the project and replace named goals with furnace fill. Interaction supplied the expressive tug and defended deliberate dumping. Replay exposed the conflict between sweeping and yank records; we now prefer per-action feedback. Its fan/fork examples respect direct links without hidden propagation. We agree on this direction; the toy-versus-puzzle balance and lasting replay remain uncertain.

The playable review must include pleasant sweeping, retained cargo, deliberate dumping, direct and cascading pulls, visible furnace progress, an earned improvement and meaningfully different deliveries at full power. Ask what players want to do next before suggesting an answer. If only the spectacular animation is enjoyable while the repeated actions are dull, revise the interaction itself. More arrangements cannot supply the missing pleasure.
