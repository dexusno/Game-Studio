# Five premises for the magnet game

Redesign step 1, 13 September 2026. **Original proposals for Klaus to choose from or combine. The names are working labels. None is selected or implemented.**

All five support the agreed side-view play, a magnet suspended from a crane or gantry, and stacked objects that can shift, tumble and swing. Ordinary scrap provides usable material or income; particular recoveries visibly advance the main project. A beam, motor housing or wheel should look like one. Where a recovered item is not magnetically responsive, its lifting attachment must be recognisable and consistent with the later interaction design.

## 1. The Last Light

**Keep your town alive through the winter.**

You are the salvage operator for a small settlement living among the remains of an abandoned industrial town. Its emergency generator is failing. An old power station could supply reliable heat and electricity, but it needs parts nobody manufactures anymore. Your crane is one of the few working machines left.

You work through ruined workshops and scrapyards, using the hanging magnet to lift heavy steel, clear unstable piles and recover useful machinery. Ordinary scrap repairs and improves your rig; selected assemblies restore the power station. A job might involve lifting a fallen steel frame away from a pump and getting the pump safely onto the transport platform.

**Visible progress:** the station changes from an empty, broken shell into a working machine. Pipes, a drive assembly and a generator return to place. Around it, the settlement gradually becomes warmer and more inhabited. Intermediate recoveries can restore individual services; the final station provides stable power for the whole town.

**Opening and ending:** open on a street going dark as the emergency generator coughs to a stop. End by starting the rebuilt station and watching light and heat return across the settlement.

**Tone and stakes:** hopeful survival in a worn world. A damaged recovery means a failed job and a valuable part lost, while reaching for extra salvage could help the settlement sooner. Exact campaign penalties, deadlines and retries are not selected here.

Keep townspeople and settlement changes as a small set of authored background scenes; this premise does not require a town-management game.

## 2. Your Own Yard

**Turn an inherited wreck of a scrapyard into a place worth keeping.**

You have inherited your family's scrapyard, its unreliable crane and an unpaid bill large enough to force its sale. You know how to operate the magnet. You need to make the yard useful again and earn enough to keep it.

Recovery contracts bring different piles and damaged machines into your work area. The magnet lets you separate heavy scrap, uncover requested parts and load them for customers. Ordinary metal pays for repairs; intact machinery earns better contracts. For example, a workshop wants a reusable motor rather than a crushed heap, so carefully removing the steel around it is worth more than collecting everything indiscriminately.

**Visible progress:** the leaking shed becomes a functioning workshop, wrecked equipment gets repaired, and the crane you use changes from a patched-up relic into a capable machine. Paid contracts fund each visible improvement and pay off the debt that threatens the yard's future.

**Opening and ending:** open with the old family sign hanging crooked above a shuttered yard. End after the final major recovery, with the repaired machinery running, workshop doors open and the yard's sign restored under your ownership.

**Tone and stakes:** grounded, independent and satisfying. You are responsible for the material you handle; wrecking a customer's requested part can fail the contract. Optional salvage offers more earnings at the risk of damaging what you were paid to retrieve.

Keep customers to simple job requests and short arrivals/departures. Property, staffing and market simulation are not necessary.

## 3. One Small Robot, One Way Home

**Build yourself a way off a planet of discarded machines.**

You are a maintenance robot whose small transport has crashed on a scrap-covered planet. The engine is ruined, but the ship's lifting gantry still works. You adapt it into a salvage crane with a hanging magnet. An old home signal still blinks on your cracked navigation screen.

Salvage sites contain wrecked vehicles, collapsed machinery and heaps of recognisable industrial parts. You use the magnet to uncover steel-framed ship assemblies and lift them free. Ordinary scrap strengthens your crane and repairs the hull; particular recoveries supply the missing propulsion, power and navigation equipment. A typical job could be removing unstable plates above a recoverable engine casing without crushing it.

**Visible progress:** the broken transport remains a recurring home scene. Holes in its hull are patched, equipment slots fill and its instruments come back to life. You can see the machine you are building and what is still missing.

**Opening and ending:** open on the robot trying—and failing—to start its wrecked ship, then turning to the working crane. End with a successful launch and the home signal answered.

**Tone and stakes:** lonely, curious and ultimately warm. Failed recoveries can destroy needed components; damaged or unstable wreckage makes greedy pulls dangerous. The sites are on the planet's surface, retaining gravity and stacked-metal behavior.

The robot can express itself with posture, lights and short sounds. This needs neither a dialogue-heavy cast nor space combat or free-flight gameplay.

## 4. Open the Harbour

**Clear the wrecks so the ships can come home.**

You operate the harbour's salvage crane after a storm has left collapsed dock machinery and a wrecked freighter blocking the channel. The town's supply vessel is waiting offshore. Clearing the passage is your job, and the magnet is powerful enough to lift wreckage that nobody can move by hand.

You dismantle obstructions a useful section at a time. Pull loose scrap out of a collapsed loading area, lift steel beams without toppling the pile, then recover heavier wreck sections onto a barge. Ordinary salvage pays for stronger lifting equipment; every required recovery physically removes part of the obstruction. The same action that earns a reward also makes the harbour more usable.

**Visible progress:** the dock becomes accessible, the broken loading structure disappears and the wreck blocking the channel gets smaller. The open route expands on the actual harbour view. Later equipment lets you tackle larger sections that were visibly beyond your first rig.

**Opening and ending:** open with the supply vessel sounding its horn beyond the blocked entrance. End by lifting the final obstruction and watching it sail into the repaired harbour to unload.

**Tone and stakes:** purposeful industrial recovery with room for dramatic lifts and a calm coastal atmosphere. Unstable sections can fall and damage a required recovery or your equipment. Salvage work can be staged in dry docks and above the waterline; underwater simulation is not required by the premise.

The town and ship provide a visible reason to work without adding harbour logistics or a shipping economy.

## 5. The Scrap Carnival

**Bring an abandoned travelling fair back to life.**

You are the mechanic who has taken responsibility for a small travelling fair that has been left to rust. Its rides are dismantled, its old organ is silent and the main wheel is missing important sections. You still have a working recovery crane and a yard full of discarded machinery.

You use its hanging magnet to find and lift usable steel parts from tangled piles and retired fairground equipment. Common scrap pays for repairs; recognisable frames, drive assemblies and wheel sections return to the attractions. A job might ask you to recover a motor assembly buried beneath old ride supports without damaging the parts it still needs.

**Visible progress:** the fair is your continuing project. Lights come on, the organ begins playing and repaired attractions start moving. The large wheel gradually acquires its missing structure, giving the whole game one obvious unfinished landmark.

**Opening and ending:** open on the silent fair and an old picture of it alive with lights. End with the main wheel turning, the gates opening and visitors arriving for the first evening.

**Tone and stakes:** playful, inventive and warm. Rough handling can ruin a needed part and fail a recovery, even though the world is cheerful. Optional valuable finds give you a reason to attempt difficult lifts.

Use a few predetermined repairs and visible assembly stages. This is not a theme-park builder, freeform engineering editor or visitor-management simulation.

## Choosing between them

| Premise | Main reason to care | What the player watches change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Last Light | Help a small community survive and recover | A power station and the settlement it supplies |
| Your Own Yard | Preserve something personal and earn independence | Your workplace and the crane you operate |
| One Small Robot, One Way Home | Help an expressive character get home | A wreck becoming a functioning spacecraft |
| Open the Harbour | Reopen a lifeline through useful physical work | The actual obstruction shrinking and the route opening |
| The Scrap Carnival | Restore something joyful that has been lost | Silent, broken attractions returning to motion |

My strongest starting candidates are **The Last Light** for a clear emotional purpose and visible construction goal, and **Open the Harbour** for the most direct connection between the magnet's everyday work and the final outcome. **One Small Robot, One Way Home** offers a central character and a particularly clear build-toward-escape structure. These are design judgments, not market predictions or owner decisions.

The risk examples establish narrative stakes only. The exact win/loss conditions, penalties, recovery/retry rules and balance belong to the next separately authorized goal. Each concept can be expressed through a brief opening, changing worksite/home view and short ending; none depends on a lore manual, an extensive cast or a large open world. Visual style remains a separate choice, and all five premises remain compatible with the planned art exploration.
