# Build-system economy proposal

Design/research review: **2026-09-13, Europe/Oslo**. Owner: progression/economy designer. This is a proposal for the new build-system goal, not a description of the implemented Extraction E1 build. No source, save, native session or shared status is changed by this document. The owner has removed development timeline constraints. The new request for a broad interacting upgrade system supersedes the old nine-purchase ceiling.

## Recommendation

Make a run a **four-site salvage expedition that ends in extracting a secured machine core**. The player assembles a capable rig through affordable early choices, expensive specializations, deliberate compromises and occasional changes of direction. A spectacular salvage operation should improve the next purchase opportunity, but should not automatically finance every item.

Use two equipped active tools and four passive sockets. A keystone occupies two passive sockets. Do not also impose the proposed eight-point equipment power budget: the same choice does not need two explanations. Site battery is an action resource, not another equipment budget. The systems designer owns exact costs and hazard rules.

**Working assumption awaiting root/owner selection:** unlocked blueprints, discovered equipment, earned records and archived rigs persist; expedition equipment and credits belong to that run. The existing paid career remains separately accessible and unchanged. An archived winning rig can be inspected and used in clearly labelled practice; it does not enter every new expedition fully equipped. This preserves earned discovery without making new runs repetitions of an already-completed shopping list.

The ultimate tool is the assembled working rig and its earned interactions, visibly capable of a final extraction that was difficult at the start. It is not a universal last purchase that replaces all earlier choices. Four sites are a representative run for testing the system, not a promise that the finished catalogue contains only four upgrades or four builds.

## The run and its visible purpose

1. **Outfit:** select one of four initially available active tools—Extraction Coil, Rail Impeller, Arc Driver or Anchor Winch—at no credit cost. All four must work independently. Vector Emitter and Relay Projector can also be selected once their blueprints are unlocked; Routes E/F explicitly assume those unlocks. Unlock criteria remain pending, and no permanent-power purchase may be required for the first victory. Start with 12 credits and a small priced shop; the first purchase changes an action or its consequence.
2. **Site 1:** use the tool on a real extraction problem, collect and refine optional valuable salvage, then dispatch the site objective. Completion unlocks a depot and pays 10 credits.
3. **Site 2:** choose between two previewed approaches with different physical opportunities. Completion pays 12 credits. Offers can deepen the existing tool or support a pivot.
4. **Site 3:** attempt a more demanding operation. Completion pays 14 credits; the last depot offers a credible keystone choice alongside useful cheaper alternatives.
5. **Final site:** free and extract the secured core. Its empty-rig mass is 20 kg against the 24 kg baseline, so buying a capacity item is not a compulsory key. Supports, obstructions, current and preservation consequences provide the operation; merely collecting a larger loose object is not a climax. Systems/level design must provide multiple complete routes.

The active HUD shows the physical objective, battery and cargo/risk. Refining shows the next actual credit milestone. The depot shows the next site's observable conditions and the player's current rig. There is no required lore, manufacturing ingredient tree, extra progression currency or overall countdown in this proposal.

## Credits, output and a payoff worth pursuing

Refined output and spendable credits have different labels. The same named metal is not displayed as spendable money at pickup and then silently reinterpreted later.

| Reward | Proposed rule | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting funds | 12 credits | Buy a useful support immediately or reserve funds for a second tool. |
| First three completed sites | 10 / 12 / 14 credits | Keeps the next shopping decision viable even without an optimized scoring build. |
| Refining milestones | Two visible thresholds per site, each paying 2 credits once | Good salvage improves actual buying power now without unbounded money multiplication. |
| Initial threshold hypotheses | Site 1: 120 / 240 output; site 2: 180 / 360; site 3: 240 / 480 | Must be retuned to reachable site material and battery budgets. Multiple strong approaches must reach them without cleaning the whole board. |
| Exceptional side reward | An authored difficult salvage opportunity may offer a named module | A new capability can justify physical risk. This is exceptional, not a free item after every clear. |
| Final extraction | Win, archive the actual rig, record operation/grade and relevant discoveries | Completes the expedition's explicit promise; no mandatory post-win grind. |

Minimum pre-finale income is `12 + 10 + 12 + 14 = 48`. Earning both refining milestones at all three sites gives 60. Resale returns some previously spent money; it is not additional generated wealth.

**Before/after example:** an Arc Driver player has 4 credits before Site 1. Refining 36 output advances the 120-output meter but crosses no milestone. Completing the physical objective leaves 14 credits. An optimized 240-output operation crosses both milestones, visibly pays 4 credits during refinement and leaves 18 after dispatch. If the stock includes Anchor Winch at 10 and Ground Clip at 5, the stronger operation can fund both transport and safe electrical termination; 14 credits can buy only one of that pair. The reward screen must show which operation earned the extra buying power. This isolated example illustrates the reward relationship; the complete reachable budget paths below are the executable scenarios.

Before smelting, show `+90 output → 120/120 reached → +2 credits`; after payment, show the next remaining threshold. Do not leave the causal relationship only in a statistics screen. After the site's credit milestones, further salvage can improve mastery records or help a physical objective, but it must be acceptable to leave residue. Never require a full clear solely to make the bonuses feel valuable. The appeal of overperformance after the cash cap remains unverified.

## Prices, offers and opportunity cost

| Purchase class | Initial price range | What the price buys |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Focused support | 4–6 | One understandable interaction or protection with a real use in the coming site. |
| Specialist / second active tool | 8–12 | A new operation or an important interaction that changes the approach. |
| Broad or powerful specialist | 14–18 | A more flexible consequence; must justify itself against two cheaper purchases. |
| Keystone | 22–26; worked examples use 24 | A strong operation built on established play; occupies two passive sockets. It is optional for victory. |
| Depot precharge | 4 | Next site's battery starts at 120 instead of 100; once, no stacking, no inventory slot, no refill during the site. Agreed with systems designer. |

Rarity determines how often an offer appears and where it can be found. Price reflects its utility, flexibility and effect—not a compulsory rarity multiplier. An uncommon specialized 5-credit Insulated Jaw can be better for a hazardous surgical route than a 24-credit demolition keystone. The UI should show a concrete operation, prerequisites, slot cost and price; a rarity colour is insufficient information.

Each initial depot stock contains four module offers plus precharge. At entry, at least **two module options** must be independently actionable and affordable; precharge does not satisfy that guarantee. One should support an installed tool and the other should supply a useful support or independently functional refit. The remaining offers can be a different-family wildcard and an aspiration. Capability, remaining-site opportunities, slot tradeoffs and the actual price all matter. The guarantee concerns initial stock, not magical price reductions after spending. Unowned prerequisites must be explicit and cannot satisfy the two-ready-choice guarantee. In particular, Conductive Tether requires a tool that creates a tether; it is not automatically useful just because Arc Driver is equipped. These authored stocks require opportunity validation; four legal identifiers are not enough.

No universal free choose-one reward follows every clear. The reward is the real cash and a guaranteed useful **priced offer**. This preserves the budget examples and leaves space for exceptional physical loot. Stock, future reward identities and random state are saved. For the first design test, use authored offer sequences; later weighted generation must preserve the same decisions. **Reroll count is zero and there is no reroll purchase in this proposal.** A possible 2-credit reroll is not part of the budget contract.

Refitting is free at depots. Selling returns `floor(actual purchase price / 2)`; display the amount before confirmation. The free starter and an exceptional found module have a zero purchase basis and give no resale cash. Selling and rebuying loses money. Duplicate modules do not silently stack unless the item's rule explicitly permits it. Credit history, equipment and the site's outcomes are one saved transaction.

## Build breadth and shared-system combinations

The six examples above describe reachable purchase paths, not the full catalogue or proven successful strategies. Six required family approaches for the final operation are:

| Family | What the player does differently | Core-operation opportunity and cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surgery | Select release joints and preserve the valuable component | Avoid destructive collateral; spend actions on precise separation. |
| Demolition | Make expendable mass into aimed impact tools | Open a protected approach; risk damaging optional delicate salvage. |
| Conduction | Establish a real conductive path and discharge unique live sources | Open/release live securing mechanisms; control branches instead of blanket immunity. |
| Tether | Place anchors, use counterweights and sequence supported movement | Extract intact assemblies; commit to spatial setup and support order. |
| Vector/repulsion | Push and rotate debris instead of always attracting it | Clear a safe corridor or move a hazard away; poor placement can block a later approach. |
| Field lattice | Place remote collection/relay regions and move salvage through them | Recover from separated locations without moving the whole load through every hazard; placement and reachable connections matter. |

Systems/level design must show a complete baseline-cost alternative for every required securing mechanism. No specific named module is a mandatory key. Vector and Relay now have priced paths and distinct proposed operations; their usefulness is not established merely by completing those budget sequences.

Two cross-family acceptance examples are explicit. **Coil + Intact Recovery + Winch/Counterweight** frees a preserved component and transports it without destroying its supports prematurely. **Arc + Ground Clip + Coil/Insulated Jaw** creates a safe electrical approach, then precisely releases the component. Both must work on the same final objective, with different physical preparation and reward consequences. Other promising hypotheses include slugs knocking conductive debris into a circuit and lattice regions catching fragments from a controlled demolition. Those are hypotheses until the systems grammar and actual geometry support them.

Catalogue extensibility means new combinations of events, valid targets and consequences—such as a severed joint causing a bounded adjacent cut or a placed tether carrying current. It does not mean hundreds of renamed percentage bonuses. Reachable build diversity requires affordable offers, compatible prerequisites, useful site situations and player choices that differ. Never publish theoretical combination counts as evidence of viable builds.

## Persistence, failure and anti-farming

Save and continue restores the exact expedition, including battery, world states, spent tool uses, credits, stock, installed modules and paid milestones. It does not reroll opportunities. A clearly named **Retry site** restores the entire atomic site-entry checkpoint, including wallet and equipment; any new credit, stock or discovery earned after that checkpoint is also rolled back. It does not restore energy while keeping rewards. Practice can use the same checkpoint without career rewards.

At a depot, the player may end the expedition and keep already-earned permanent discoveries/records under the chosen persistence policy. Failure does not delete existing blueprints or the old career. Exact new discovery timing—paid at site dispatch versus kept immediately—is an integration decision; use dispatch for valuable permanent rewards so deliberate failed-site farming cannot become the main progression loop.

Refining or ability-trigger rewards require unique authored salvage/source identities. Repeatedly picking up, dropping, severing an already severed joint or relaying the same object must not mint fresh output or credits. A source may participate in an impressive bounded chain according to its rule, but cannot trigger itself indefinitely. This is an accounting invariant, not a hidden nerf that activates when the player builds well.

## Peer critique, exclusions and next evidence

Agreed with the systems designer: two active/four passive slots; keystones use two passive sockets; no redundant equipment power meter; four initial starters plus two unlockable families; priced compatible offers rather than automatic free items; 4-credit next-site precharge; thirty provisional module identities and six-family direction. Agreed with the critic: explicit reset semantics, atomic retries, visible capped credit rewards, multiple ways to reach the milestones and permission to leave unprofitable residue. Root challenged fixed pay that made smelting decorative; the two immediate-credit refining milestones are the response. The critic's B stock repair is adopted with the systems designer's explicit surviving-body Fuse/Rebound ordering.

Unresolved risks are material. The owner has not yet selected the persistence model. A finite cash cap can flatten excitement after both milestones. A physical final core can still become a disguised key check. Four sites may give too few decisions for a late pivot. A keystone can crowd out more interesting cheap interactions. The rejected B stock exposed why buying a bad fit must not become the only alternative to keeping cash; the revised stock needs real opportunity testing. Large deterministic chains can be impressive but unreadable, and the six families require genuinely different geometry/state interactions that the current inert tray does not implement.

Do not add loot-box purchases, real spending, a crafting ingredient economy, passive income timers, a prestige tax or mandatory permanent power grind to solve these issues. Do not silently rewrite the owner's existing save into a temporary rig. No implementation is authorized by this specialist document alone; root integrates the selected promise and contracts first.

The smallest evidence-producing iteration is a shared extraction site plus an opening depot, a later pivot depot and a final-operation fixture, using the actual priced paths above and at least two contrasting tool families. Test whether the player can predict an interaction, use it, recognize the extra reward and voluntarily choose a different approach. Extend to all six families before claiming broad build viability. Design agreement, arithmetic and successful compilation cannot answer the playtest question: **“Did your purchases make you want to solve the next site differently, and did the resulting operation feel worth earning?”**

The earlier integration critique replaced Route D / depot 2's unused Insulated Jaw with Ratchet Pawl 6.
